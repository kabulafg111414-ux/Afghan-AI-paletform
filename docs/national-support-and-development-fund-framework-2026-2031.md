# National Support and Development Fund Framework (2026–2031)

**Document status:** Proposed design framework for discussion and technical review. It is not a legally established fund, public appeal, bank account, grant programme, government decision, or evidence that money has been received or distributed.

## 1. Purpose

This framework describes how a future National Support and Development Fund could transparently mobilize, receive, govern, allocate, monitor, and report voluntary contributions for clearly defined public-benefit programmes in Afghanistan. It is designed to complement—not replace—lawful public services, humanitarian coordination, and existing protection systems.

The fund must not begin collecting or distributing money until its legal host, authority, registration, banking arrangements, tax treatment, sanctions and AML/CFT obligations, safeguarding procedures, and independent oversight have been verified by qualified professionals and relevant authorities.

## 2. Proposed programme windows

1. **Family resilience:** time-limited support for households facing verified severe hardship, with clear eligibility and referral pathways.
2. **Children and education:** school access, learning materials, safe learning spaces, and referrals for protection concerns.
3. **Disability inclusion:** accessibility improvements, assistive-device referral or support, inclusive education, and participation.
4. **Youth skills and livelihoods:** needs-based vocational training, apprenticeships, and job-placement partnerships.
5. **Community recovery:** small, community-prioritized improvements to water, sanitation, health access, and essential local infrastructure.
6. **Emergency response:** tightly defined, time-limited response to shocks, coordinated with competent humanitarian actors.

Each window requires a separate approved programme note, budget, eligibility criteria, safeguards, delivery partners, and results framework before launch.

## 3. Beneficiary protection and eligibility

- Publish plain-language eligibility criteria, geographic scope, exclusions, application routes, and appeal procedures.
- Use proportionate verification; do not demand unnecessary sensitive documents.
- Assess vulnerability using transparent, consistently applied criteria and documented human review.
- Do not promise assistance before eligibility and funding are confirmed.
- Provide accessible routes for people with disabilities and people with limited literacy or connectivity.
- Prohibit fees, kickbacks, political or personal favoritism, coercion, discrimination, and retaliation.
- Separate application decisions from fundraising, procurement, and payment approval.
- Establish safe referral protocols for child-protection, gender-based violence, medical, and other specialist cases; do not collect detailed case narratives in a public platform.

## 4. Governance and separation of duties

A legally authorized governing body should approve strategy, annual budgets, risk appetite, programme windows, and public reporting. Membership, appointment rules, conflicts declarations, term limits, and removal procedures must be published.

Minimum independent functions:
- **Programme committee:** reviews needs, eligibility policy, and results.
- **Finance and audit committee:** oversees budgets, reconciliations, controls, and audit findings.
- **Safeguarding and complaints focal point:** handles confidential concerns and referrals.
- **Independent auditor:** appointed under a documented process and with access to relevant records.
- **Management team:** executes approved plans and reports performance.

No individual should control the full chain of beneficiary selection, procurement, payment authorization, and reconciliation. Related-party transactions require disclosure, independent review, and recorded approval.

## 5. Financial controls

Before operations, adopt written policies for:
- authorized bank and payment channels in the fund's verified legal name;
- dual authorization and role-based access;
- donor restrictions and permitted-use tracking;
- budget controls and documented approvals;
- receipts, invoices, contracts, and proof of delivery;
- monthly bank and ledger reconciliations by a person independent of payment initiation;
- cash minimization and documented exceptions;
- asset registers and physical verification;
- fraud reporting, investigation, recovery, and referral;
- record retention and secure destruction schedules.

Never publish personal account details, QR codes, payment instructions, or fundraising claims until ownership, authorization, and reconciliation controls have been independently verified. Never route donations through an individual's personal account.

## 6. Donations and donor due diligence

Classify contributions by source, restriction, currency, date received, fees, and intended programme. Apply risk-based due diligence consistent with applicable law, including screening and escalation where required. Decline or return funds where source, conditions, legality, or reputational risk cannot be satisfactorily resolved.

Donor conditions must not override beneficiary rights, impartial eligibility, procurement integrity, or the fund's public-benefit purpose. No donor should receive preferential beneficiary selection or access to confidential personal data.

## 7. Allocation and procurement

- Publish programme budgets, selection methodology, procurement thresholds, and approval authorities.
- Use competitive procurement where feasible; document exceptions and price reasonableness.
- Publish contract awards and beneficial ownership information where lawful and safe.
- Require supplier conflict declarations, delivery verification, and sanctions/legal checks as applicable.
- Use milestone-based payments and independent acceptance for significant works or services.
- Maintain an accessible complaints route for applicants, beneficiaries, staff, suppliers, and communities.

## 8. Privacy, data security, and safeguarding

Collect only data necessary for eligibility, delivery, audit, and lawful reporting. Establish a privacy notice, lawful basis, access controls, encryption, retention periods, breach response, and secure deletion process before collecting personal data.

Public reports must be aggregated and suppress small groups or details that could identify a beneficiary. Do not publish names, precise household locations, identity documents, health records, disability details, or children's images without a valid legal basis and informed, freely given consent where consent is appropriate. A child's best interests and safeguarding take priority over publicity.

Digital applications must include a non-digital alternative and must not imply that an online form guarantees assistance.

## 9. Complaints, appeals, and whistleblowing

Provide confidential, accessible channels independent of programme selection and payment teams. Publish response standards after capacity is established. Allow anonymous reports where practical. Protect complainants and whistleblowers from retaliation, preserve evidence, document case handling, and refer allegations to competent independent bodies when appropriate.

Complaints data should be reported in aggregate, including received, resolved, pending, and overdue cases, without exposing identities.

## 10. Results and public accountability

For every programme, publish an approved results plan containing:
- objective and theory of change;
- baseline, indicator definition, source, and date;
- approved target and target year;
- actual result, reporting date, and verification status;
- expenditure against approved budget;
- outputs and outcome evidence;
- limitations, missing data, and corrective actions.

Distinguish money **pledged**, **received**, **available**, **committed**, **disbursed**, and **spent**. These terms are not interchangeable. Publish no amount as received or spent without reconciled records. Report administrative costs transparently and explain allocation methodology.

## 11. Minimum public dashboard

| Area | Suggested public measure |
|---|---|
| Finance | Reconciled receipts, expenditure, balances, and audit status |
| Reach | Verified households or participants supported, reported in aggregate |
| Education | Learner participation and continuation, where safely measurable |
| Disability inclusion | Accessible services and reasonable accommodation delivered |
| Skills and livelihoods | Training completion and verified employment outcomes |
| Community recovery | Completed assets/services and independent acceptance |
| Accountability | Procurement disclosures, audit actions, and complaint resolution |
| Safeguarding | Policy compliance and aggregate incident-handling status, without case details |

All measures require defined methodology, data owner, reporting period, and verification. Do not invent baselines, targets, beneficiary counts, or impact claims.

## 12. Implementation gates

**Gate 0 — Feasibility:** legal-host assessment, needs assessment, stakeholder consultation, and risk analysis.

**Gate 1 — Legal and institutional readiness:** verified legal entity and mandate, governing documents, authorized signatories, banking, compliance review, and insurance assessment where relevant.

**Gate 2 — Operational readiness:** approved policies, staffing, safeguarding, privacy, financial systems, complaints channels, procurement rules, and independent audit arrangements.

**Gate 3 — Pilot approval:** costed programme note, verified delivery partner, baseline, selection rules, funding confirmation, and monitoring plan.

**Gate 4 — Pilot review:** independent financial and programme review, beneficiary feedback, corrective actions, and public report.

**Gate 5 — Scale decision:** documented evidence that controls and outcomes are adequate; otherwise redesign, pause, or close the programme.

No fundraising or beneficiary intake should be presented as active before the relevant gates are passed.

## 13. Risk register

| Risk | Minimum mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fraud or diversion | Dual controls, reconciliation, audit, protected reporting |
| Legal or regulatory breach | Qualified legal review and documented compliance checks |
| Ineligible or unsafe funding | Risk-based donor due diligence and refusal/return procedure |
| Elite capture or favoritism | Published criteria, independent review, appeals, audit trail |
| Harm to children or vulnerable people | Safeguarding policy, trained personnel, safe referrals |
| Data breach or re-identification | Data minimization, access control, encryption, aggregation |
| Delivery partner failure | Due diligence, written agreements, milestones, verification |
| Currency and inflation volatility | Currency policy, scenario planning, transparent reporting |
| Public misinformation | Evidence-based communications and clear status labels |

## 14. First 90-day preparation plan

- **Days 1–30:** legal feasibility, stakeholder mapping, needs assessment, and risk review.
- **Days 31–60:** draft governance, financial, procurement, safeguarding, privacy, and complaints policies; identify independent professional reviewers.
- **Days 61–90:** select a narrowly scoped pilot only if legal and operational gates are met; prepare a costed budget, baseline, partner due diligence, and public disclosure template.

This is a preparation sequence, not a promise that the fund will launch within 90 days.

## 15. Required annexes before launch

1. Legal opinion and registration/mandate documents.
2. Governing charter and committee terms of reference.
3. Conflict-of-interest and code-of-conduct forms.
4. Financial management and procurement manuals.
5. Donor due-diligence and restricted-funds policy.
6. Safeguarding, child-protection, and referral procedures.
7. Privacy notice, data map, retention schedule, and breach plan.
8. Beneficiary eligibility, appeals, and complaints procedures.
9. Pilot programme design, budget, baseline, and indicator dictionary.
10. Independent audit and evaluation terms of reference.

## 16. Disclaimer

This document is a proposed framework only. It does not establish a fund, authorize fundraising, create an entitlement to assistance, verify a bank account or payment channel, confirm donors or partners, or represent approval by any government, institution, or international organization. All legal, financial, regulatory, safeguarding, and operational arrangements require independent verification and formal authorization before implementation.
