# Transparency, Accountability and Anti-Corruption Framework
## Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program | 2026–2031

**Document status:** Proposed program-level governance framework  
**Purpose:** Establish practical controls for integrity, public accountability, procurement transparency and responsible use of funds.  
**Important limitation:** This framework is a proposal, not proof that a legal entity, public authority, oversight body, bank account, audit arrangement or complaint mechanism has been established. Implementation requires lawful authority, competent personnel, independent providers and applicable approvals.

---

## 1. Core commitments

The program should operate according to the following commitments:

1. Every source and use of funds is recorded and traceable.
2. No person may approve, procure, receive and reconcile the same transaction alone.
3. Procurement decisions are documented, competitive and reviewable, subject to lawful exceptions.
4. Conflicts of interest are declared, assessed and managed.
5. Contracts, awards, variations and payments are supported by evidence.
6. Independent assurance is separated from project delivery.
7. Communities and affected people have safe ways to raise concerns.
8. Personal, medical, financial and other sensitive information is protected.
9. Suspected fraud, coercion, diversion or retaliation is escalated promptly.
10. Public claims about partners, funding, results and approvals are accurate and verifiable.

## 2. Governance and separation of duties

A proposed governance structure should distinguish policy oversight, management, procurement, finance, technical delivery and independent assurance.

| Function | Core responsibility | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| Governing / oversight body | Approve strategy, risk appetite, budgets and major decisions | Directly process routine payments |
| Program management | Coordinate approved work plans and report delivery | Override procurement or audit independence |
| Project owner | Define need, outputs, technical scope and asset handover | Evaluate its own work as the sole verifier |
| Procurement function | Run fair tendering and maintain procurement records | Approve its own contract payments |
| Finance function | Budget control, accounting, reconciliation and reporting | Certify physical completion alone |
| Technical supervisor | Verify quantities, quality and milestones | Release funds without authorized finance approval |
| Independent auditor / monitor | Test controls, transactions and results | Design or operate the controls it audits |
| Complaints focal point | Receive and safely route complaints | Share complainant identity without need or authority |

Appointments, authority limits, reporting lines and removal procedures must be documented. The program should publish the names and roles of authorized officials only after their identities and consent are verified and disclosure is safe and lawful.

## 3. Financial management controls

### 3.1 Budget authorization
- Every project must have an approved budget, funding source, cost breakdown and accountable budget owner.
- Budget transfers and reallocations require written approval under a published delegation matrix.
- Commitments must not exceed available, authorized funds.
- Multi-year liabilities and future operating costs must be disclosed.

### 3.2 Banking and cash
- Use accounts held by a legally authorized entity, not personal accounts, for program funds.
- Verify the account holder, beneficial ownership where applicable, authorized signatories and bank mandate.
- Apply dual authorization for payments above approved thresholds.
- Minimize cash transactions; document and reconcile any permitted petty cash.
- Reconcile bank statements monthly and independently review unreconciled items.
- Never publish full account numbers, credentials or security details in public reports.

### 3.3 Accounting and supporting records
Each transaction should include:
- Unique transaction and project reference.
- Date, amount, currency and exchange rate.
- Payee identity and verified legal details.
- Approved budget line and authorization.
- Contract, purchase order or other lawful basis.
- Invoice, delivery note and evidence of receipt.
- Technical certification for works or services, where relevant.
- Payment proof and accounting entry.
- Tax and withholding treatment, where applicable.

Maintain an auditable ledger and a controlled document repository with role-based access, version history, backup and retention rules.

## 4. Procurement integrity framework

Procurement should be planned and conducted in a way that supports fair competition, value for money and equal treatment.

### 4.1 Procurement planning
- Publish an annual or project procurement plan where safe and lawful.
- Define specifications around outcomes and performance, not a preferred supplier.
- Estimate costs independently before tendering.
- Select a procurement method under approved rules and document the rationale.
- Avoid artificial contract splitting to bypass thresholds.

### 4.2 Competition and evaluation
- Provide equal access to tender information and adequate response time.
- Record questions, clarifications and addenda and share them with all bidders.
- Use pre-disclosed evaluation criteria and scoring methods.
- Require evaluators to declare conflicts before accessing bids.
- Keep signed evaluation records and reasons for award or rejection.
- Record single-source or emergency procurement exceptions with legal basis, justification, price reasonableness and post-award review.

### 4.3 Contract disclosure
Subject to safety, privacy, commercial confidentiality and applicable law, publish:
- Tender or procurement reference.
- Project and contract title.
- Procurement method and reason for any exception.
- Awarded supplier's legal name.
- Award date, contract value and currency.
- Scope, duration and key deliverables.
- Contract amendments and variations.
- Completion status and final paid amount.

Do not publish protected personal data, bank details, security-sensitive information or confidential bid content.

## 5. Conflict of interest and beneficial ownership

All board members, staff, advisers, evaluators and relevant contractors should disclose actual, potential or perceived conflicts.

Controls should include:
- Initial declaration before appointment or participation.
- Annual updates and event-triggered updates.
- A confidential register managed by an authorized compliance officer.
- Recusal from discussions, scoring, decisions and access to restricted information where a conflict exists.
- Independent review of related-party transactions.
- Verification of supplier identity and beneficial ownership to the extent permitted by law.
- Documented decisions on mitigation, recusal or disqualification.

A conflict declaration is not automatically evidence of misconduct; failure to disclose or improper influence must be assessed fairly and documented.

## 6. Anti-fraud and misconduct response

The program should maintain a written protocol for allegations involving bribery, kickbacks, collusion, theft, diversion, falsified records, coercion, retaliation, exploitation or misuse of assets.

Response steps:
1. Receive and securely log the concern.
2. Assess immediate safety, evidence-preservation and retaliation risks.
3. Restrict access to sensitive records on a need-to-know basis.
4. Refer the matter to an independent and competent reviewer.
5. Preserve relevant records and maintain a chain of custody.
6. Pause affected payments or activities where authorized and proportionate.
7. Refer to competent authorities when legally required and safe.
8. Document findings, corrective action and closure.
9. Protect complainants, witnesses and subjects from unfair disclosure or retaliation.

Do not publicly identify an accused person or complainant based on an unverified allegation. Apply due process and confidentiality.

## 7. Independent audit and monitoring

The program should plan for three complementary assurance layers:

**Management controls**
- Routine reconciliations, approvals, stock checks, contract monitoring and management review.

**Independent financial and compliance audit**
- Risk-based testing of accounts, procurement, payroll, assets, grants, contracts and compliance with donor conditions.
- Auditor independence, conflict checks, access to records and direct reporting to the oversight body.

**Independent technical and results verification**
- Site inspections, engineering tests, delivery verification, beneficiary feedback and outcome validation.
- Use qualified independent specialists for high-value or technically complex works.

Audit frequency and scope should be based on applicable law, donor agreements, project risk and materiality. Publish audit summaries and management responses when lawful and safe; do not claim an audit has occurred until a signed report exists.

## 8. Public disclosure and reporting

Create a public project register with a page for each approved project.

Suggested fields:
- Project ID, title, sector and location at a safe level of detail.
- Sponsor and implementing entity.
- Project status and approval stage.
- Approved budget and funding source, clearly distinguishing pledged, committed, received and disbursed amounts.
- Procurement plan, tender notices and contract awards.
- Milestones, schedule and verified progress.
- Variations, delays and reasons.
- Safeguard commitments and implementation status.
- Output and outcome indicators, baseline and reporting period.
- Audit or independent-monitoring summary.
- Grievance mechanism and aggregate complaint statistics.

### Financial status terminology
Use these terms consistently:
- **Announced:** Public statement only; not necessarily legally binding.
- **Pledged:** Stated intention to provide support, subject to terms.
- **Committed:** Binding obligation documented and authorized.
- **Received:** Funds cleared into the authorized project account.
- **Allocated:** Funds assigned to an approved budget or purpose.
- **Disbursed:** Payment made and recorded.
- **Expended:** Payment supported by records for goods, works or services.
- **Audited:** Subject to a completed audit with a signed report.

Never add pledged amounts to cash received or describe an expression of interest as an investment commitment.

## 9. Community participation and grievance mechanism

A safe, accessible and independent grievance mechanism should be designed before works begin.

It should:
- Accept complaints in appropriate local languages and accessible formats.
- Offer more than one channel where feasible, including in-person and remote options.
- Allow anonymous reporting where practicable.
- Explain confidentiality limits and realistic response timelines.
- Prohibit retaliation and provide escalation routes.
- Provide safe referral pathways for allegations involving violence, exploitation, abuse or other sensitive harms.
- Record cases using restricted identifiers and minimize personal data.
- Track receipt, acknowledgement, assessment, action, referral and closure.
- Publish only aggregated, non-identifying statistics.

The mechanism is not a substitute for courts, regulators, emergency services or legally mandated reporting.

## 10. Environmental and social accountability

Each project should:
- Screen environmental and social risks before site selection and procurement.
- Consult affected communities in a meaningful, accessible and culturally appropriate manner.
- Assess land access, displacement, livelihood impacts and compensation where relevant.
- Protect workers through occupational health and safety measures.
- Include child safeguarding and prevention of exploitation and abuse.
- Provide equitable access for underserved groups, including people with disabilities, where feasible.
- Monitor water, waste, pollution, biodiversity and climate risks as relevant.
- Track mitigation actions, responsible parties and deadlines.

Consultation must not be coercive. Sensitive participation data must be protected and not exposed where disclosure could create risk.

## 11. Asset management and prevention of leakage

Maintain an asset register for vehicles, machinery, equipment, buildings, materials and other controlled assets.

Required controls:
- Unique asset ID, description, location, custodian and acquisition details.
- Physical verification at risk-based intervals.
- Documented transfer, repair, loss, disposal and write-off.
- Restricted access to stores and warehouses.
- Independent checks of high-value inventory.
- Handover documents and named maintenance responsibility at project completion.

## 12. Data protection and cybersecurity

- Collect only data necessary for a defined and lawful purpose.
- Use role-based access and strong authentication for financial and personal records.
- Encrypt sensitive records in transit and at rest where technically feasible.
- Maintain secure backups and test restoration.
- Log access to critical financial systems.
- Establish incident reporting, containment and recovery procedures.
- Set retention and secure deletion rules consistent with legal obligations.
- Never place passwords, API keys, personal identification numbers or bank credentials in public repositories.

## 13. Risk register and escalation

Maintain a live risk register with:
- Risk description and category.
- Likelihood and impact.
- Inherent and residual risk ratings.
- Mitigation and control owner.
- Due date and status.
- Trigger for escalation.
- Evidence of closure.

Escalate immediately when there is credible evidence of fund diversion, serious safety threats, retaliation, major procurement manipulation, material accounting discrepancies, unlawful land access or serious safeguard breaches.

## 14. Implementation roadmap

| Stage | Priority action | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|
| Before program launch | Confirm legal entity, authority, governance and account controls | Verified legal and banking documentation |
| Before project procurement | Approve procurement rules, delegation matrix and conflict declarations | Signed policies and registers |
| Before construction | Establish safeguards, grievance channel and monitoring plan | Approved instruments and operational channel |
| During delivery | Publish project register and periodic financial/progress reports | Dated public reports and supporting records |
| At least annually, or as required | Independent audit and risk-based technical verification | Signed reports and management responses |
| At handover | Verify assets, warranties, operator and maintenance funding | Handover and asset-register records |
| After completion | Evaluate outcomes and publish lessons | Independent evaluation report |

## 15. Minimum policy package to adopt

Before implementation, the authorized entity should formally approve:
1. Governance charter and delegation of authority.
2. Financial management and accounting manual.
3. Procurement and contract management policy.
4. Conflict-of-interest and gifts policy.
5. Anti-fraud, whistleblowing and non-retaliation policy.
6. Audit and independent-monitoring terms of reference.
7. Environmental and social safeguards framework.
8. Grievance and safeguarding procedures.
9. Data protection and cybersecurity policy.
10. Records retention and public disclosure policy.

Each policy should identify its legal basis, owner, approval date, version, review cycle and responsible implementation roles.

## 16. Final disclosure

This document is a proposed framework for future adoption and implementation. It does not establish that controls are operational, that funds are held, that audits have been completed, or that any public institution or international organization has endorsed the program. All claims of implementation must be supported by current documentary evidence.
