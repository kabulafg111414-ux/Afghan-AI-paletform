# Indicative Investment Portfolio and Budget Framework
## Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program | 2026–2031

**Document status:** Preliminary planning framework — illustrative estimates only  
**Currency:** United States dollars (USD)  
**Price basis:** No detailed engineering, market quotations or site surveys have been completed for this framework  
**Purpose:** Support early discussion, prioritization and preparation of project-specific feasibility studies

> **Critical financial disclaimer:** The figures in this document are hypothetical planning ranges, not verified cost estimates, approved budgets, funding commitments, offers of investment, or forecasts of guaranteed returns. They must not be used for procurement, lending, fundraising claims or financial commitments. Actual costs may differ materially by location, scope, access, security, land, design, market prices, exchange rates, taxes, safeguards and implementation conditions. No government or institution is represented as having approved or funded these projects.

---

## 1. Portfolio Approach

The program should begin with a limited, geographically defined pilot portfolio rather than attempting nationwide construction in every sector at once. Each project should be selected through documented needs assessment, technical feasibility, legal due diligence, environmental and social screening, affordability analysis and a credible operating plan.

The preliminary portfolio below illustrates the possible order of magnitude for a group of pilot projects across six priority areas. It does not represent a nationwide reconstruction budget.

## 3. Common Program Costs and Contingency

For early scenario analysis only, a provisional allowance of **15%–25% of the six-sector subtotal** may be tested for project preparation, design, supervision, independent monitoring, program management, safeguards, audits and risk contingencies.

This is not a standard percentage or an approved overhead. Each component must be separately estimated, justified and disclosed. Contingency should be risk-based and should not be treated as discretionary spending.

## 5. Illustrative $1 Trillion Long-Term Scenario

A $1 trillion figure, if retained in strategic discussions, must be described only as a hypothetical long-term national-scale scenario over multiple phases and sectors—not as the cost of this six-project pilot, not as a financing requirement validated by research, and not as funds secured.

Any national-scale scenario would require:
- A national infrastructure and service baseline.
- Province- and district-level needs assessment.
- A comprehensive inventory of existing assets and rehabilitation needs.
- Sector strategies, demand forecasts and climate-risk analysis.
- Macroeconomic, fiscal and debt-sustainability analysis.
- Prioritization based on economic and social returns, affordability and implementation capacity.
- Independent review and transparent public consultation.

No allocation of a trillion-dollar scenario should be published as an official estimate without this evidence.

## 6. Project-Level Cost Structure

Each project budget must separately disclose:

1. Feasibility studies, surveys and detailed design.
2. Land access, acquisition or compensation, where lawful and applicable.
3. Civil works, equipment, materials and installation.
4. Taxes, duties, transport and insurance.
5. Environmental and social mitigation measures.
6. Construction supervision and independent quality assurance.
7. Training, commissioning and initial operating support.
8. Annual operations and maintenance.
9. Asset replacement and lifecycle costs.
10. Contingency, with the underlying risk assumptions.

All estimates should state their date, currency basis, price source, tax treatment, exchange-rate assumptions, physical quantities and confidence level.

## 7. Financial and Economic Appraisal

Every project should be evaluated using methods appropriate to its purpose.

**Public-benefit projects:** Assess economic benefits, affordability, distributional effects, avoided costs, service quality and fiscal sustainability. A public clinic, community water system or access road may create substantial social value without generating commercial cash revenue.

**Commercially viable projects:** Assess realistic demand, revenue, operating costs, working capital, financing costs, taxes, replacement reserves and downside scenarios. Private investors should receive no implied guarantee of revenue or repayment.

**Required sensitivity tests:** Construction cost escalation; delays; lower demand; changes in energy or input prices; exchange-rate movements; climate and security disruption; lower-than-expected collection or utilization; and maintenance cost increases.

Economic benefits and financial cash flows must be reported separately to avoid presenting social benefits as cash available to repay investors.

## 8. Potential Financing Mix — Subject to Due Diligence

Potential financing instruments may include:
- Grants for technical assistance, public goods and essential services.
- Concessional financing only after debt sustainability and repayment capacity are independently assessed.
- Private investment for projects with credible commercial cash flows and transparent risk allocation.
- Public-private partnerships only after affordability and value-for-money review.
- Blended finance where grant and commercial components have distinct purposes and transparent terms.
- Community participation only where voluntary, lawful, safe and equitable.

The program should not borrow in the name of communities or public entities without verified authority, lawful approvals and clear repayment responsibility.

## 9. Disbursement and Financial Controls

Recommended controls include:
- Dedicated project accounts and documented signatory authority.
- Approved budgets and segregation of duties.
- Competitive procurement and documented bid evaluation.
- Payments tied to independently verified milestones.
- Beneficial-ownership and conflict-of-interest checks where lawful.
- Contract variation and change-order registers.
- Regular reconciliations and independent audits.
- Public reporting of commitments, disbursements, contract awards and progress, subject to legitimate privacy and security limits.
- A documented process to suspend payments when fraud, diversion or serious safeguard concerns arise.

No donations, grants or investments should be solicited or accepted through personal accounts on behalf of the program unless a lawful entity, authority, financial controls and donor terms have been verified.

## 10. Five-Year Phasing Framework

| Period | Indicative workstream | Financial gate |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | Governance, baseline studies, candidate locations and pre-feasibility | Release only approved preparation funds |
| 2027 | Full feasibility, designs, permits, safeguards and procurement readiness | No construction commitment before approval gates |
| 2028 | Selected pilot implementation | Contract payments against verified milestones |
| 2029–2030 | Independent evaluation and selective scaling | Expand only where performance and affordability are demonstrated |
| 2031 | Completion, handover, maintenance and final evaluation | Confirm lifecycle funding and asset ownership |

The schedule is indicative and depends on legal authority, access, technical findings, financing and procurement.

## 11. Investment Readiness Scorecard

A project should not be presented as investment-ready until the following evidence is available:

| Readiness requirement | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| Legal sponsor | Registration, authority and governance documents |
| Verified need | Baseline data and documented stakeholder consultation |
| Technical feasibility | Options analysis, surveys, design and independent review |
| Site and land | Verified rights, access and permits |
| Safeguards | Environmental and social screening and required assessments |
| Financial case | Cost estimate, lifecycle budget and sensitivity analysis |
| Commercial case, if applicable | Demand validation, revenue assumptions and downside case |
| Procurement | Approved plan, transparent specifications and evaluation rules |
| Implementation | Qualified operator, schedule, supervision and risk register |
| Sustainability | Operations, maintenance, replacement and handover arrangements |

## 12. Immediate Work Plan

1. Select no more than a small initial group of candidate sites using transparent criteria.
2. Commission independent technical and market assessments.
3. Develop quantity-based cost estimates for each candidate project.
4. Verify land, permits, institutional authority and safeguards.
5. Prepare project-specific economic and financial models.
6. Obtain independent review of assumptions and estimates.
7. Publish revised budgets with a clear estimate class and limitations.
8. Approach prospective partners with evidence-based, project-specific requests.

## 13. Conclusion

This framework provides an initial structure for discussing a phased reconstruction portfolio. Its indicative ranges are not a substitute for engineering estimates or feasibility studies. The program's credibility must rest on independently verified needs, lawful authority, realistic lifecycle costs, transparent procurement, strong safeguards and public accountability.

**No amount in this document is represented as committed, raised, approved or available.**
