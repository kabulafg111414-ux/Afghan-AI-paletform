# Master Implementation Roadmap — 30 Chapters
## Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program | 2026–2031

**Document status:** Strategic implementation architecture — proposed, subject to legal authority, feasibility, consultation and funding  
**Planning horizon:** 2026–2031  
**Purpose:** Organize the proposed program into 30 connected chapters, with accountable roles, outputs, indicators, decision gates and a phased implementation sequence.

> **Status and integrity notice:** This roadmap is a planning framework, not an approved national plan, government decision, financing commitment or confirmation that any institution has agreed to participate. All project locations, budgets, targets, partners and implementation responsibilities must be verified before being represented as final. Numerical targets are intentionally not invented.

---

## 1. Program delivery architecture

The 30 chapters are grouped into six delivery phases:

| Phase | Period (indicative) | Main purpose | Gate to proceed |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Institutional readiness | 2026 | Legal status, governance, baseline, priorities and consultation | Authority, governance and evidence verified |
| II. Project preparation | 2026–2027 | Feasibility, design, safeguards, costing and operating plans | Independent project appraisal completed |
| III. Pilot procurement and mobilization | 2027–2028 | Competitive procurement, financing agreements and readiness | Funds authorized, permits and contracts valid |
| IV. Pilot implementation | 2028–2029 | Deliver selected projects and monitor quality | Milestones independently verified |
| V. Evaluation and selective scale-up | 2029–2030 | Evaluate results and expand only proven interventions | Benefits, affordability and capacity demonstrated |
| VI. Handover and sustainability | 2030–2031 | Asset transfer, maintenance, final evaluation and public reporting | Operator, lifecycle funding and records confirmed |

The dates are indicative and may shift according to legal, technical, access, financing and procurement conditions.

## 2. Thirty-chapter structure

### Chapter 1 — Program identity, mandate and legal status
**Objective:** Establish exactly what the program is, who proposes it and what authority exists.  
**Core actions:** Verify legal entity and registration; define ownership and mandate; distinguish proposal from approved public policy; document authorized signatories and limitations.  
**Lead role:** Authorized governing body / legal adviser.  
**Outputs:** Legal-status note, mandate statement, authority matrix and public disclaimer.  
**Indicators:** Current registration and authority documents available; all public claims reviewed for accuracy.  
**Timing:** Phase I.  
**Gate:** No external commitment or fundraising representation before authority is verified.

### Chapter 2 — National needs assessment and baseline
**Objective:** Identify priority needs using reliable and appropriately disaggregated evidence.  
**Core actions:** Review existing datasets; identify data gaps; conduct ethical field assessment; consult affected communities; document uncertainty and access limitations.  
**Lead role:** Planning and research team with independent technical support.  
**Outputs:** Baseline report, needs map at a safe level of detail and evidence register.  
**Indicators:** Priority sectors have dated baselines, sources and documented limitations.  
**Timing:** Phase I–II.  
**Gate:** No quantified impact claims without baseline evidence.

### Chapter 3 — Governance and institutional accountability
**Objective:** Define decision rights, oversight, separation of duties and reporting lines.  
**Core actions:** Establish governance charter; delegation matrix; board and committee terms; risk oversight; meeting and decision records.  
**Lead role:** Governing body and governance adviser.  
**Outputs:** Governance charter, authority matrix and decision register.  
**Indicators:** Each material decision has an authorized owner and recorded approval.  
**Timing:** Phase I.  
**Gate:** No project procurement without approved governance and authority.

### Chapter 4 — Public finance and budget management
**Objective:** Ensure realistic budgeting, controlled commitments and traceable expenditure.  
**Core actions:** Adopt budgeting and accounting rules; distinguish pledged, committed, received, allocated, disbursed and expended funds; establish reconciliation and reporting.  
**Lead role:** Finance director / qualified financial controller.  
**Outputs:** Financial manual, chart of accounts, project budgets and reporting templates.  
**Indicators:** Reconciliations completed; material variances explained; funds traceable to approved purposes.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** No payment without lawful authority, supporting evidence and budget availability.

### Chapter 5 — Procurement and contract management
**Objective:** Promote fair competition, value for money and enforceable delivery obligations.  
**Core actions:** Approve procurement rules; prepare procurement plans; publish tenders where appropriate; evaluate bids transparently; manage variations and claims.  
**Lead role:** Procurement unit, independently overseen.  
**Outputs:** Procurement policy, tender documents, evaluation records and contract register.  
**Indicators:** Procurement files complete; exceptions justified; contracts monitored against milestones.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** No contract award before required approvals, integrity checks and funding authorization.

### Chapter 6 — Anti-corruption, audit and public transparency
**Objective:** Prevent, detect and respond to fraud, conflicts and misuse of resources.  
**Core actions:** Conflict declarations; whistleblowing and non-retaliation process; independent audit; asset register; public project register and corrective-action tracking.  
**Lead role:** Compliance function and independent auditor.  
**Outputs:** Integrity policy, audit plan, complaints protocol and public disclosure register.  
**Indicators:** Declarations and audit actions tracked; complaints handled under confidentiality rules.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Escalate credible allegations; pause affected transactions where authorized and proportionate.

### Chapter 7 — Water security and irrigation
**Objective:** Improve reliable, equitable and sustainable water access for communities and agriculture.  
**Core actions:** Assess hydrology and water rights; identify rehabilitation options; evaluate watershed and irrigation efficiency; define local maintenance arrangements.  
**Lead role:** Water engineering team and legally authorized water stakeholders.  
**Outputs:** Water-sector baseline, feasibility studies, designs and maintenance plans.  
**Indicators:** Water availability, delivery reliability, service coverage and maintenance performance measured against baseline.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** No construction without water-resource, rights, land and safeguard review.

### Chapter 8 — Renewable energy and essential-service power
**Objective:** Improve reliable and sustainable power for priority services and viable economic activity.  
**Core actions:** Assess demand and resource; compare grid, solar, storage and hybrid options; plan tariffs or subsidy; establish operator and replacement funding.  
**Lead role:** Energy technical team and authorized utility/operator.  
**Outputs:** Energy feasibility, design, connection plan and lifecycle cost model.  
**Indicators:** Service hours, reliability, connections and operating cost measured.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Confirm ownership, grid permissions, safety and long-term O&M.

### Chapter 9 — Essential health services
**Objective:** Strengthen access to functioning and adequately supplied essential health services.  
**Core actions:** Assess facility readiness, staffing, referral pathways, equipment, utilities, medicine supply and recurrent financing.  
**Lead role:** Qualified health-sector planner and authorized service operator.  
**Outputs:** Health service gap assessment, facility plans, equipment and staffing plans.  
**Indicators:** Facility functionality, service availability, utilization and referral performance.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Do not treat construction as proof of improved health outcomes; confirm staffing and supplies.

### Chapter 10 — Education, learning and school infrastructure
**Objective:** Support safe, accessible and effective learning environments.  
**Core actions:** Assess school access, learning conditions, teacher capacity, sanitation, accessibility, learning materials and maintenance.  
**Lead role:** Education planning team and authorized education stakeholders.  
**Outputs:** Education needs assessment, school improvement plans and learning-support model.  
**Indicators:** Attendance, retention, safe access, facility readiness and learning measures where valid.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Ensure child safeguarding, safe access and operational staffing.

### Chapter 11 — Vocational training and employment pathways
**Objective:** Align practical skills training with verified employer and market demand.  
**Core actions:** Conduct labor-market assessment; co-design curricula; equip training; establish apprenticeships and job follow-up.  
**Lead role:** Skills and workforce development team with employers.  
**Outputs:** Skills strategy, competency standards, training plans and employer agreements.  
**Indicators:** Completion, competency, placement and sustained employment tracked separately.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** No claim of jobs created based only on training enrolment.

### Chapter 12 — Agriculture, food security and farmer services
**Objective:** Improve agricultural productivity, resilience and farmer access to services and markets.  
**Core actions:** Assess crop systems, inputs, extension, water, soil, storage and market constraints; pilot locally suitable support.  
**Lead role:** Agricultural technical team and producer organizations.  
**Outputs:** Agriculture baseline, extension plan, climate-smart options and market assessment.  
**Indicators:** Yield, farm income, input efficiency and resilience indicators selected by crop and location.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Validate agronomic suitability, water impacts and market demand.

### Chapter 13 — Agro-processing, storage and cold chain
**Objective:** Reduce post-harvest losses and improve value addition where commercial demand is demonstrated.  
**Core actions:** Verify volumes and seasonality; assess energy, logistics, food safety, buyer demand and operator model.  
**Lead role:** Agribusiness and engineering team.  
**Outputs:** Market study, facility design, business plan and operator arrangements.  
**Indicators:** Utilization, loss rates, throughput, producer access and operating viability.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** No facility investment without credible supply, demand and maintenance arrangements.

### Chapter 14 — Transport, roads and logistics connectivity
**Objective:** Improve safe and reliable access to services and markets.  
**Core actions:** Assess traffic, road condition, drainage, geotechnical issues, safety, climate risk and maintenance.  
**Lead role:** Transport engineering team and authorized road asset owner.  
**Outputs:** Corridor assessment, engineering designs, cost estimates and maintenance plans.  
**Indicators:** Seasonal access, journey time, safety and road condition.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Confirm land, environmental impacts, road authority and lifecycle maintenance.

### Chapter 15 — Housing, urban services and resilient settlements
**Objective:** Support safe, serviced and resilient settlement improvements where legally and socially appropriate.  
**Core actions:** Assess tenure, planning, water, sanitation, drainage, waste, access and disaster exposure; avoid involuntary displacement.  
**Lead role:** Urban planning team and relevant lawful authorities.  
**Outputs:** Settlement plans, service assessments and safeguards.  
**Indicators:** Service access, safety, tenure-related grievances and maintenance performance.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** No works on disputed land or without lawful planning and safeguard review.

### Chapter 16 — Water, sanitation, hygiene and waste services
**Objective:** Improve safe water, sanitation, hygiene and waste management services.  
**Core actions:** Assess service chains, water quality, wastewater, solid waste, operator capacity and affordability.  
**Lead role:** WASH and municipal-services technical team.  
**Outputs:** Service baseline, system designs, operating model and water-quality plan.  
**Indicators:** Service continuity, quality compliance, safe disposal and user access.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Confirm operator, public-health safeguards and recurrent financing.

### Chapter 17 — Digital infrastructure and public-service technology
**Objective:** Use appropriate digital systems to improve service access, records and accountability.  
**Core actions:** Assess connectivity, power, accessibility, interoperability, cybersecurity, data protection and lifecycle support.  
**Lead role:** Digital transformation and cybersecurity specialists.  
**Outputs:** Digital architecture, data governance rules, service prototypes and support plan.  
**Indicators:** System availability, adoption, accessibility, incident response and service outcomes.  
**Timing:** Phase I–V.  
**Gate:** No sensitive-data platform without privacy, security and lawful data-governance review.

### Chapter 18 — Private-sector development and small businesses
**Objective:** Support a competitive business environment and responsible local enterprise growth.  
**Core actions:** Identify regulatory and market barriers; map supply chains; support transparent business services and access to markets.  
**Lead role:** Enterprise development team and relevant business associations.  
**Outputs:** Market constraints assessment, enterprise support design and supplier-development plan.  
**Indicators:** Firm survival, productivity, formal employment and market access where measurable.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Avoid grants or procurement advantages without transparent eligibility and controls.

### Chapter 19 — Trade, regional connectivity and market access
**Objective:** Improve lawful trade facilitation and access to regional and domestic markets.  
**Core actions:** Assess border and logistics bottlenecks, standards, storage, transport links and trade information.  
**Lead role:** Trade and logistics specialists with relevant authorized institutions.  
**Outputs:** Trade bottleneck assessment and practical reform/project proposals.  
**Indicators:** Clearance time, logistics cost and market access where data is reliable.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** Recommendations must respect applicable laws, agreements and institutional mandates.

### Chapter 20 — Tourism, culture and heritage
**Objective:** Support responsible cultural heritage protection and tourism opportunities where safe and feasible.  
**Core actions:** Assess heritage condition, community rights, conservation needs, visitor access, safety and local benefit.  
**Lead role:** Heritage and tourism specialists with custodians and communities.  
**Outputs:** Heritage assessment, conservation proposals and responsible tourism plans.  
**Indicators:** Conservation condition, local participation, visitor management and benefit distribution.  
**Timing:** Phase II–V.  
**Gate:** No activity that damages heritage, displaces custodians or creates unacceptable safety risks.

### Chapter 21 — Environment, biodiversity and climate resilience
**Objective:** Integrate environmental protection and climate resilience into every sector.  
**Core actions:** Screen climate and environmental risks; assess biodiversity, water and pollution; design mitigation and monitoring.  
**Lead role:** Environmental and climate specialists.  
**Outputs:** Program safeguards framework, climate-risk assessments and mitigation plans.  
**Indicators:** Mitigation completion, resource efficiency, resilience and compliance.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Serious unmitigated impacts require redesign or no-go decision.

### Chapter 22 — Disaster risk reduction and emergency preparedness
**Objective:** Reduce exposure and strengthen readiness for natural and other emergencies.  
**Core actions:** Map hazards; assess critical infrastructure vulnerability; prepare continuity plans; strengthen local response capacity.  
**Lead role:** Disaster-risk specialists and authorized emergency stakeholders.  
**Outputs:** Risk profiles, resilience standards and preparedness plans.  
**Indicators:** Critical assets assessed, plans tested and corrective actions completed.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Avoid publishing sensitive security information or unverified hazard claims.

### Chapter 23 — Social inclusion, disability access and community participation
**Objective:** Ensure project design considers access, safety, dignity and meaningful participation.  
**Core actions:** Identify barriers; consult diverse affected groups safely; design accessible facilities and grievance channels; monitor equitable access.  
**Lead role:** Social safeguards and community engagement team.  
**Outputs:** Inclusion plan, consultation records and accessibility requirements.  
**Indicators:** Participation quality, accessible services and resolved barriers.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Consultation must be voluntary, safe, accessible and privacy-protecting.

### Chapter 24 — Labor standards, occupational safety and child safeguarding
**Objective:** Protect workers, children and communities throughout project delivery.  
**Core actions:** Set labor requirements; contractor codes; occupational safety plans; child-labor prevention; safeguarding and referral processes.  
**Lead role:** Labor and safeguarding specialists with contract managers.  
**Outputs:** Labor management procedures, safety plans and incident protocols.  
**Indicators:** Training, inspections, incident reporting and corrective-action closure.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Serious abuse or safety concerns require immediate protective action and appropriate escalation.

### Chapter 25 — Human capacity and institutional strengthening
**Objective:** Develop the skills and systems needed to plan, deliver and maintain projects.  
**Core actions:** Assess capacity; define training pathways; mentor local professionals; transfer operating knowledge and records.  
**Lead role:** Capacity development team and participating operators.  
**Outputs:** Capacity assessment, training plan and institutional handover package.  
**Indicators:** Competency assessments, retention and independent ability to operate assets.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Training outputs must be linked to demonstrated institutional needs.

### Chapter 26 — Monitoring, evaluation, learning and impact
**Objective:** Measure performance honestly and use evidence to improve decisions.  
**Core actions:** Define baselines, indicators, data owners, reporting frequency, independent verification and evaluation questions.  
**Lead role:** Independent monitoring and evaluation function.  
**Outputs:** Results framework, dashboard specification, evaluation plan and lessons register.  
**Indicators:** Data completeness, verification coverage, outcome evidence and corrective actions.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Do not publish impact claims unsupported by verifiable data.

### Chapter 27 — Risk management, legal compliance and dispute resolution
**Objective:** Identify and manage delivery, financial, legal, political, operational and reputational risks.  
**Core actions:** Maintain risk register; assess legal compliance; define escalation; review contracts and dispute pathways.  
**Lead role:** Risk manager and qualified legal advisers.  
**Outputs:** Enterprise risk framework, compliance register and dispute protocol.  
**Indicators:** High risks have owners, mitigations, review dates and documented escalation.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** Unresolved critical legal or integrity risks block implementation.

### Chapter 28 — Financing strategy and investment readiness
**Objective:** Match projects with suitable, affordable and transparent financing.  
**Core actions:** Complete project appraisal; assess grant, equity, debt and PPP suitability; disclose contingent liabilities; test affordability.  
**Lead role:** Finance and investment advisory team, subject to independent review.  
**Outputs:** Project financing plans, due-diligence package and financing-status register.  
**Indicators:** Funding status evidenced; lifecycle costs and liabilities disclosed.  
**Timing:** Phase II–VI.  
**Gate:** No borrowing, guarantee or investment offer without lawful authority and approvals.

### Chapter 29 — International cooperation and partnership development
**Objective:** Conduct credible, project-specific engagement with potential technical and financing partners.  
**Core actions:** Map mandates and eligibility; prepare non-binding concept notes; conduct due diligence; negotiate only through authorized representatives.  
**Lead role:** Authorized partnership office and legal adviser.  
**Outputs:** Partner matrix, contact register, approved concept notes and reviewed draft agreements.  
**Indicators:** Engagement stages documented accurately; no unsupported partner claims.  
**Timing:** Phase I–VI.  
**Gate:** No logo, endorsement or partnership announcement without written authorization.

### Chapter 30 — Completion, asset handover and long-term sustainability
**Objective:** Ensure completed projects remain safe, functional, maintained and accountable after program support ends.  
**Core actions:** Commission assets; verify quality; transfer ownership and records; fund maintenance; evaluate outcomes; publish close-out report.  
**Lead role:** Asset owner, operator and independent completion reviewer.  
**Outputs:** Handover certificates, asset register, warranties, O&M funding plan and final evaluation.  
**Indicators:** Assets operational; operator trained; maintenance funded; outstanding defects tracked.  
**Timing:** Phase V–VI.  
**Gate:** No project close-out without verified handover and sustainable operating arrangements.

## 3. Cross-program management system

Every chapter and project should use the same management cycle:

1. **Plan:** Define the need, options, scope, costs, owner, schedule and risks.
2. **Approve:** Verify legal authority, safeguards, affordability and independent appraisal.
3. **Procure:** Run authorized, documented and fair procurement.
4. **Deliver:** Track milestones, quality, safety, costs and community feedback.
5. **Verify:** Independently check physical progress, payments and results.
6. **Report:** Publish accurate progress and financial status, protecting sensitive information.
7. **Learn:** Correct weaknesses and scale only interventions supported by evidence.
8. **Handover:** Confirm asset ownership, operator, maintenance and final evaluation.

| Employment | Temporary FTEs, permanent jobs, verified sustained placements | Payroll and independent follow-up |
| Services | Availability, reliability, utilization and quality | Service operator records |
| Inclusion | Accessible participation and service access | Safeguard monitoring |

| Sustainability | O&M funding, asset condition and operator readiness | Asset owner and inspections |

- Handover and maintenance requirements.

## 6. Decision gates and stop rules

A chapter or project must not advance merely because its planned date has arrived.

**Gate A — Authority:** legal entity, mandate, land rights and approvals verified.  
**Gate B — Evidence:** need, baseline, technical options and stakeholder input documented.  
**Gate C — Feasibility:** design, costs, safeguards, operating model and affordability independently reviewed.  
**Gate D — Financing:** source, conditions, cash flow, liabilities and approvals verified.  
**Gate E — Procurement:** fair process, contract, conflict checks and authorization complete.  
**Gate F — Delivery:** milestones, safety, quality, safeguards and payments independently checked.  
**Gate G — Handover:** asset owner, operator, records, warranties and maintenance funding confirmed.  
**Gate H — Evaluation:** outcomes and lessons independently assessed and disclosed.

Pause, redesign or cancel where there is unresolved unlawful land access, lack of authority, unmitigated serious harm, unsustainable liabilities, material fraud risk, absence of an operator or unreliable cost and baseline evidence.

## 7. First-year priority sequence

The first year should focus on readiness rather than announcing a large number of construction projects.

| Sequence | Priority | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verify legal status, ownership and authority | Current legal documents and authorized mandate |
| 2 | Approve governance, finance, procurement and integrity policies | Signed and version-controlled policies |
| 3 | Establish baseline and consultation methodology | Baseline plan and evidence register |
| 4 | Select a small number of candidate pilot projects | Published selection criteria and decision records |
| 5 | Commission independent pre-feasibility and safeguards work | Terms of reference and independent reports |
| 6 | Prepare quantity-based costs and lifecycle models | Reviewed cost estimates and O&M plans |
| 7 | Complete partner eligibility and due diligence | Verified partner mapping and contact log |
| 8 | Publish first readiness and risk report | Dated public report with limitations |

## 8. Reporting calendar

Subject to the capacity and obligations of the eventual legal entity:
- Monthly internal project and financial review.
- Quarterly program performance and risk review.
- Periodic public progress update, with safe disclosure.
- Annual independent financial and compliance audit, or as required by law and funding agreements.
- Independent technical verification at critical milestones.
- Completion evaluation and post-completion sustainability review.

## 9. Required annexes for a complete operational plan

The following controlled annexes should be prepared and approved before implementation:
1. Legal mandate and governance charter.
2. Sector baseline and data-quality report.
3. Project prioritization scorecard and decision log.
4. Detailed project concept notes and feasibility reports.
5. CAPEX, OPEX and lifecycle budget models.
6. Procurement plan and contract register.
7. Environmental and social safeguards instruments.
8. Financial management and audit manual.
9. Risk, compliance and grievance registers.
10. Results framework and monitoring plan.
11. Partner due-diligence and financing-status register.
12. Asset management and handover procedures.

## 10. Final status disclosure

This 30-chapter roadmap is a proposed structure for planning and evaluation. It does not establish that the program has been approved, that any chapter has been implemented, that a budget has been authorized, or that any institution has committed resources. Actual delivery depends on verified legal authority, independent evidence, consultation, feasibility, safeguards, financing and competent implementation arrangements.
