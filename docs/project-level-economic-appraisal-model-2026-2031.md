# Project-Level Economic Appraisal Model
## Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program | 2026–2031

**Status:** Standard appraisal template for project preparation  
**Use:** Complete separately for each proposed project and candidate location  
**Important:** This document contains formulas and evidence requirements, not verified project results. Do not publish estimated jobs, returns, beneficiaries or economic impact until the underlying data has been collected and independently reviewed.

---

## 1. Purpose and decision rule

This model provides a consistent way to assess whether a proposed project is:
- needed and technically feasible;
- legally and institutionally deliverable;
- affordable over its full lifecycle;
- likely to produce measurable public or commercial benefits;
- environmentally and socially responsible; and
- ready for procurement, financing or implementation.

A high score must never override a failed legal, safety, land-rights, safeguard or affordability requirement. Projects that fail a mandatory requirement should be paused or redesigned, regardless of their weighted score.

## 5. Financial model — commercial projects only

Where a project is expected to earn revenue, prepare a year-by-year cash-flow model.

Core calculations:

- Gross revenue = units sold or services delivered × verified price.
- Net operating cash flow = collected revenue − cash operating costs − taxes and required reserves.
- Free cash flow = net operating cash flow − capital expenditure.
- Net present value (NPV) = sum of discounted project cash flows, including the initial investment.
- Internal rate of return (IRR) = discount rate at which NPV equals zero.
- Debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR) = cash available for debt service ÷ scheduled principal and interest.

Every model must disclose its discount rate, inflation basis, tax treatment, financing assumptions, collection rate, working-capital needs and residual-value assumptions. Report both nominal and real assumptions consistently. Do not calculate or advertise an IRR where cash flows are not sufficiently supported.

Public-benefit projects should not be forced into a commercial-return model. Their social and economic benefits must be assessed separately.

## 6. Economic and social benefits

Identify benefits that can be measured and attributed to the project. Avoid double counting.

| Benefit area | Example indicator | Baseline required? | Evidence method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access to services | Travel time / service availability | Yes | Household or service survey |
| Water and agriculture | Reliable water delivery / irrigated area | Yes | Hydrological and field measurements |
| Energy | Hours of reliable service / connections | Yes | Meter and outage records |
| Health | Service readiness / utilization / referral access | Yes | Facility records and independent checks |
| Skills and employment | Completion, placement and sustained employment | Yes | Cohort tracking and employer verification |
| Agriculture and markets | Post-harvest loss / storage use / market access | Yes | Producer and market surveys |
| Transport | Journey time / seasonal passability / road condition | Yes | Engineering and traffic surveys |
| Inclusion | Access by women, persons with disabilities and underserved groups, where safely measurable | Yes | Ethical, privacy-protecting monitoring |

For each benefit, state the measurement unit, baseline date, target rationale, data owner, frequency, verification method and limitations.

## 7. Employment measurement protocol

Report jobs transparently and distinguish:
1. **Temporary construction jobs:** People engaged during construction; report duration and full-time-equivalent (FTE) methodology.
2. **Direct permanent jobs:** Ongoing positions operated by the project.
3. **Indirect jobs:** Supplier or value-chain employment supported by the project; disclose the estimation method and avoid presenting modelled jobs as directly verified.
4. **Training participants:** Report separately from jobs.
5. **Sustained employment outcomes:** Verify employment at defined follow-up intervals, such as 6 and 12 months, where feasible and safe.

Do not add temporary job counts across months as if they were unique permanent jobs. Publish sex, age or disability disaggregation only where lawful, safe, voluntary and privacy-protecting.

## 8. Sensitivity and stress testing

At minimum, test:
- CAPEX increase of 10%, 20% and 30%;
- implementation delay of 6 and 12 months;
- OPEX increase of 10%, 20% and 30%;
- demand or utilization 10%, 20% and 30% below the base case;
- revenue collection below the expected rate, where relevant;
- exchange-rate depreciation and imported-input cost changes;
- fuel, electricity and key-material price shocks;
- climate events, access constraints and interruption scenarios.

These are test shocks, not forecasts. Project teams should add sector-specific risks and justify the selected ranges. Show the effect on total cost, service continuity, affordability, cash flow and debt-service capacity where relevant.

## 9. Weighted project selection scorecard

Score each criterion from 0 to 5:
- 0 = no evidence / unacceptable;
- 1 = very weak;
- 2 = weak;
- 3 = adequate;
- 4 = strong;
- 5 = independently verified / excellent.

Weighted points = (criterion score ÷ 5) × criterion weight.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence expected |
|---|---:|---|
| Demonstrated need and beneficiary value | 15 | Baseline, consultation, demand evidence |
| Technical feasibility and readiness | 15 | Surveys, options analysis, design |
| Economic and social benefits | 15 | Cost-benefit or cost-effectiveness evidence |
| Lifecycle affordability and O&M | 15 | CAPEX, OPEX and funded maintenance plan |
| Legal authority, land and permits | 10 | Verified rights, approvals and sponsor authority |
| Environmental and social performance | 10 | Screening, assessments and mitigation |
| Implementation and procurement capacity | 10 | Qualified team, procurement plan and schedule |
| Climate resilience and sustainability | 5 | Climate-risk assessment and resilience design |
| Transparency and monitoring | 5 | Audit, reporting and grievance arrangements |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Suggested interpretation after mandatory gates are passed:
- 80–100: Consider for detailed preparation or independent appraisal.
- 65–79: Improve evidence and redesign weak areas before appraisal.
- Below 65: Do not advance without substantial revision.

The score is a screening aid, not an investment approval or guarantee of success.

## 10. Mandatory no-go / pause conditions

Pause the project and escalate for independent review if any of the following applies:
- Sponsor authority or legal status cannot be verified.
- Land ownership, access, resettlement or compensation issues remain unresolved.
- Required permits or approvals are absent.
- Serious environmental, community safety or human-rights risks lack an acceptable mitigation plan.
- The project has no credible operator or maintenance funding.
- Procurement presents unresolved conflicts of interest, collusion or material integrity concerns.
- The financing plan creates unsustainable debt or undisclosed liabilities.
- Baseline information or cost estimates are materially unreliable.
- Community consultation is unsafe, coercive or not meaningfully accessible.

## 11. Six-sector appraisal prompts

### A. Water security and irrigation
Assess hydrology, seasonal availability, water rights, downstream users, sedimentation, canal losses, climate resilience, maintenance responsibility and equitable distribution. Define water-service indicators before selecting works.

### B. Renewable energy and essential-service power
Assess resource quality, demand profile, grid connection, storage needs, tariffs or subsidy arrangements, battery replacement, operator skills, safety and end-of-life disposal. Verify who pays recurring costs.

### C. Essential health services
Assess catchment population, existing facility capacity, workforce availability, referral pathways, medicine and supply continuity, infection prevention, utilities, accessibility and recurrent public financing. Construction alone is not a health outcome.

### D. Vocational skills and employment
Validate employer demand before selecting trades. Measure completion, competency, placement, earnings where safely measurable, and sustained employment. Separate training enrolment from actual employment.

### E. Agricultural processing and cold chain
Assess crop volumes, seasonality, farmer organization, energy reliability, food safety, storage utilization, transport links, buyer commitments and commercial operating margins. Avoid building capacity without verified demand.

### F. Transport and market connectivity
Assess traffic, all-season access, drainage, geotechnical conditions, road safety, maintenance funding, environmental impacts and alternatives. Prioritize lifecycle cost and access benefits, not only kilometres built.

## 12. Independent review and approval gates

| Gate | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Gate 0 — Concept | Is the need credible and aligned with program priorities? |
| Gate 1 — Pre-feasibility | Are there viable options and a plausible delivery path? |
| Gate 2 — Feasibility | Are technical, legal, safeguard, economic and financial analyses complete? |
| Gate 3 — Investment readiness | Are design, cost, funding, procurement and operating plans independently reviewed? |
| Gate 4 — Contract award | Are approvals, competition, integrity checks and funding authorization complete? |
| Gate 5 — Implementation | Are milestones, quality, safeguards and disbursements independently monitored? |
| Gate 6 — Completion and handover | Are assets tested, records transferred and maintenance funded? |
| Gate 7 — Outcome evaluation | Are outcomes independently measured against baseline and targets? |

No gate should be deemed passed solely because a document has been drafted. The underlying evidence and responsible approval must be recorded.

## 13. Standard project appraisal submission checklist

A complete submission should include:
- Project information sheet and verified sponsor documents.
- Needs assessment and baseline.
- Options analysis and feasibility report.
- Site, land, rights and permit documentation.
- Detailed design and bill of quantities.
- Independent cost estimate and estimate date.
- CAPEX, OPEX and lifecycle financing plan.
- Economic appraisal and, where appropriate, financial model.
- Environmental and social screening and required plans.
- Climate-risk and resilience assessment.
- Procurement and implementation plan.
- Risk register and mitigation owners.
- Monitoring, evaluation and grievance plan.
- Independent review comments and responses.
- Written approval record for the next gate.

---

## 14. Required disclosure

This is a reusable methodology template. It does not establish that any specific project has been studied, approved, financed or found viable. No project-specific cost, job figure, beneficiary number, revenue, return or economic impact should be claimed until supported by current, traceable evidence and appropriate independent review.
