# Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program
## Investor and Development Partner Concept Proposal | 2026–2031

**Prepared for:** Prospective development partners, responsible investors, humanitarian and development institutions  
**Program status:** Concept-stage proposal for discussion and due diligence  
**Proposed geographic scope:** Afghanistan, subject to access, legal review, security assessment and verified local needs  
**Proposed period:** 2026–2031  
**Program sponsor:** Independent program initiative; institutional sponsorship and legal operating entity to be confirmed

> **Important notice:** This document is a concept proposal, not an approved government plan, investment prospectus, feasibility study, funding commitment or solicitation to purchase securities. No partnership, endorsement, grant, loan, guarantee or funding from any government, the United Nations, World Bank, donor, investor or other institution is implied. All projects require independent feasibility work, legal review, safeguards assessment and written approvals before implementation.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program (2026–2031) is a proposed, multi-sector framework to identify, prepare and support practical reconstruction and livelihood projects. It focuses on six interconnected areas: water and irrigation; renewable and reliable energy; essential health services; vocational skills and employment; agricultural processing and cold-chain infrastructure; and transport and market connectivity.

The program is designed around a staged approach. It does not assume that all projects can or should begin at once. Each proposed project would pass through needs verification, technical and economic feasibility, legal and land due diligence, environmental and social assessment, financing approval, transparent procurement, implementation and independent monitoring.

The intended result is a pipeline of well-prepared projects that can be considered individually by qualified public authorities, communities, development partners and responsible private investors. Project selection should prioritize measurable public benefit, affordability, local ownership, maintainability and equitable access.

## 2. Context and Rationale

Afghanistan's reconstruction needs are interconnected. Reliable water supports agriculture and public health; energy enables clinics, schools, businesses and processing facilities; skills development helps people access employment; and transport links connect producers to markets and essential services.

A credible reconstruction pipeline must therefore combine infrastructure with human capacity, service delivery and long-term operations. It must also recognize constraints that vary by province and district, including security, access, climate exposure, institutional capacity, land rights, market demand and availability of qualified operators.

This proposal does not present unverified national statistics. Baselines and target values must be established through current, independently verifiable data during project preparation.

## 3. Program Vision, Mission and Principles

**Vision:** Communities across Afghanistan benefit from resilient infrastructure, stronger livelihoods and more reliable essential services.

**Mission:** Develop a transparent pipeline of technically sound, socially responsible and financially realistic reconstruction projects.

**Guiding principles**
- Evidence-led planning and local consultation.
- Do-no-harm, conflict sensitivity and equitable access.
- Transparent budgeting, procurement and reporting.
- Respect for applicable law, land rights and contractual obligations.
- Environmental sustainability and climate resilience.
- Inclusion of women, youth, people with disabilities and underserved communities, consistent with applicable law, safety and meaningful access.
- No project proceeds without a credible operating and maintenance plan.

## 4. Strategic Objectives

1. Improve reliable access to water, irrigation and climate-resilient agricultural services.
2. Expand dependable energy for essential services and productive use.
3. Strengthen access to basic, quality-assured health services.
4. Improve employable skills and pathways to safe, dignified work.
5. Reduce post-harvest losses and improve agricultural value addition.
6. Improve safe and cost-effective access between communities, services and markets.
7. Build transparent project governance, monitoring and public accountability.

## 5. Proposed Flagship Project Areas

The following are project concepts, not approved construction packages. Locations, designs, beneficiaries, costs and schedules remain to be established through feasibility studies.

### 5.1 Water Security and Irrigation Rehabilitation

**Concept:** Assess and rehabilitate selected irrigation canals, water-control structures, community water systems and watershed measures.

**Potential activities:** Hydrological surveys; rehabilitation of priority canal sections; water-user association support; efficient irrigation demonstrations; watershed restoration; water-quality testing where relevant.

**Expected benefits:** More reliable water access, improved agricultural productivity and reduced vulnerability to drought.

**Preparation requirements:** Water availability and rights assessment; engineering design; downstream impact review; land access; climate-risk analysis; community operation and maintenance arrangements.

**Illustrative indicators:** Hectares with improved reliable irrigation; households with improved safe water access where included; water-system functionality; water-use efficiency; maintenance performance.

### 5.2 Renewable Energy and Essential-Service Power

**Concept:** Develop context-appropriate solar, mini-grid, hybrid or other technically suitable systems for clinics, schools, productive facilities and underserved communities.

**Potential activities:** Energy demand audits; solar and storage feasibility; grid-interconnection assessment; mini-grid business models; technician training; spare-parts and maintenance arrangements.

**Expected benefits:** More reliable electricity for essential services and local economic activity.

**Preparation requirements:** Resource assessment; demand and affordability study; tariff and subsidy analysis; battery lifecycle and disposal plan; operator selection; safety and technical standards.

**Illustrative indicators:** Hours of reliable service; facilities connected; system uptime; cost per unit of delivered energy; maintenance response time.

### 5.3 Essential Health-Service Strengthening

**Concept:** Support selected primary-care facilities and outreach services based on verified gaps and service-readiness assessments.

**Potential activities:** Facility rehabilitation where justified; essential equipment; reliable power and water; supply-chain improvements; staff training; referral and outreach systems.

**Expected benefits:** Improved availability and continuity of essential health services.

**Preparation requirements:** Health-authority coordination; workforce and service mapping; medicine and equipment supply plans; infection prevention; clinical governance; safeguards for patient privacy.

**Illustrative indicators:** Facility readiness; essential service availability; stock-out frequency; referral completion; service utilization disaggregated where safe and lawful.

### 5.4 Vocational Skills, Apprenticeships and Employment Pathways

**Concept:** Develop demand-led training linked to local employers and viable sectors.

**Potential activities:** Labour-market assessment; competency-based courses; apprenticeships; business and digital skills; job placement support; recognition of prior learning where feasible.

**Potential training fields:** Electrical installation and solar maintenance; irrigation and water-system maintenance; food processing; construction trades; logistics; digital skills; repair services.

**Expected benefits:** Better alignment between skills and local labour demand, with pathways to employment or self-employment.

**Preparation requirements:** Employer validation; training-provider assessment; occupational safety; accessible training design; transparent participant selection; graduate follow-up.

**Illustrative indicators:** Enrolment and completion; competency assessment results; employment or self-employment after training; employer satisfaction; participation of underserved groups.

### 5.5 Agricultural Processing and Cold-Chain Development

**Concept:** Assess and support commercially and technically viable aggregation, storage, processing and cold-chain facilities near production areas and markets.

**Potential activities:** Value-chain studies; collection centres; packhouses; cold storage; food-safety systems; processing equipment; producer organization support; market linkages.

**Expected benefits:** Reduced post-harvest losses, improved product quality and increased value retained by producers.

**Preparation requirements:** Supply-volume and seasonality analysis; buyer commitments; energy and water reliability; food-safety compliance; operating model; affordability and utilization forecasts.

**Illustrative indicators:** Volume handled; storage utilization; post-harvest loss rates; producer sales; facility uptime; share of operating costs covered by revenue.

### 5.6 Transport, Logistics and Market Connectivity

**Concept:** Identify high-priority local access links and logistics bottlenecks that materially improve access to markets and essential services.

**Potential activities:** Road-condition surveys; spot rehabilitation; drainage and slope protection; road-safety measures; market access improvements; logistics studies.

**Expected benefits:** More reliable travel, lower transport friction and improved access to services and markets.

**Preparation requirements:** Traffic and demand assessment; engineering and geotechnical studies; land acquisition and resettlement screening; road-safety audit; lifecycle maintenance funding.

**Illustrative indicators:** Road sections meeting defined service standards; seasonal accessibility; travel-time changes measured against baseline; road-safety outcomes; maintenance compliance.

## 6. Project Preparation and Approval Gateways

Every project should pass the following gateways before funds are committed:

1. **Needs validation:** Confirm the problem, affected population, local priorities and available baseline data.
2. **Pre-feasibility:** Compare options, demand, technical suitability, preliminary costs and alternatives.
3. **Full feasibility and safeguards:** Complete engineering, economic, financial, environmental, social, climate and security assessments as appropriate.
4. **Legal and institutional due diligence:** Verify land tenure, permits, counterpart authority, procurement rules, ownership and dispute-resolution arrangements.
5. **Financing and affordability approval:** Confirm funding sources, full lifecycle costs, affordability, foreign-exchange exposure and contingent liabilities.
6. **Procurement readiness:** Prepare specifications, bidding documents, evaluation criteria, conflict-of-interest declarations and contract-management plans.
7. **Implementation authorization:** Confirm safeguards, supervision, insurance where applicable, grievance channels and operating arrangements.
8. **Completion and evaluation:** Independently verify outputs, disclose results and establish funded maintenance responsibilities.

Projects that fail a gateway should be redesigned, deferred or discontinued.

## 7. Implementation Roadmap, 2026–2031

**Phase 1 — Set-up and evidence (2026):** Confirm legal sponsor and governance; map stakeholders; select candidate locations transparently; establish baseline data; commission initial feasibility and safeguards studies.

**Phase 2 — Design and pilot preparation (2027):** Complete priority designs; secure permits and land arrangements; validate operators and market demand; prepare procurement packages; seek project-specific financing approvals.

**Phase 3 — Pilot implementation (2028):** Implement only projects that have passed all approval gates; publish procurement and progress information; monitor costs, quality, safeguards and service performance.

**Phase 4 — Independent review and selective expansion (2029–2030):** Evaluate pilot performance; correct weaknesses; scale only projects with demonstrated need, performance, affordability and operating capacity.

**Phase 5 — Consolidation and transition (2031):** Complete independent evaluation; transfer assets and responsibilities to qualified operators; secure maintenance plans; publish lessons and a follow-on investment pipeline.

Dates are indicative planning windows and may change after feasibility, approvals, funding and access assessments.

## 8. Governance and Institutional Arrangements

A credible delivery structure should be legally established before funds are received or contracts awarded. The final structure may include:

- **Program steering body:** Provides strategic oversight, approves program criteria and reviews performance. Membership and authority must be legally documented.
- **Independent program management unit:** Coordinates preparation, schedules, risk registers, reporting and partner engagement.
- **Project implementation entities:** Qualified public, private, community or non-profit operators selected under applicable law and transparent procedures.
- **Independent technical supervision:** Verifies design, construction quality, service delivery and contract milestones.
- **Independent audit and evaluation:** Reviews financial controls, procurement, results and compliance.
- **Community engagement and grievance mechanism:** Enables safe, accessible complaints and feedback, with documented response timelines.

No institution should be described as a partner, sponsor or approving authority until written authorization is obtained.

## 9. Financing Strategy

Financing should be project-specific and based on feasibility, affordability and risk allocation. Potential instruments for consideration include:

- Grants for public goods, technical assistance, feasibility studies and services that cannot reasonably recover costs.
- Concessional loans only where debt sustainability and repayment capacity are independently assessed.
- Responsible private investment for commercially viable activities with transparent contracts and fair risk allocation.
- Public-private partnerships only where value-for-money, affordability, contingent liabilities and public-interest protections are demonstrated.
- Community contributions only where voluntary, safe, equitable and lawful; essential services must not be made inaccessible to households unable to contribute.
- Blended finance where grant and commercial components have clear purposes, transparent terms and independently verified additionality.

**No funding amount is represented as secured.** Capital expenditure, operating expenditure, contingencies, taxes, land costs, foreign-exchange assumptions and lifecycle maintenance must be estimated separately for each project. A consolidated budget should be published only after feasibility and independent review.

### Financial model to be completed for each project

| Cost or funding item | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Capital expenditure | Quantity-based engineering estimate and market testing |
| Operating expenditure | Staffing, energy, supplies, maintenance and administration |
| Contingency | Risk-based allowance with documented assumptions |
| Revenue or cost recovery | Demand study, affordability analysis and sensitivity tests |
| Grant or concessional component | Written funding terms and eligibility confirmation |
| Private capital | Investor due diligence and binding financing documentation |
| Lifecycle funding | Funded maintenance, replacement and end-of-life plan |

## 10. Environmental and Social Safeguards

Each project should be screened proportionately and assessed under applicable national law and relevant partner standards. Safeguards should address:

- Land ownership, access, acquisition and involuntary resettlement risks.
- Water availability, downstream users and ecosystem impacts.
- Biodiversity, pollution, waste and construction impacts.
- Labour rights, occupational health and safety, and contractor conduct.
- Community health and safety, road safety and security risks.
- Inclusion, accessibility, affordability and protection from exploitation.
- Cultural heritage and chance-find procedures.
- Climate resilience and disaster-risk reduction.

A project should not proceed where severe impacts cannot be avoided, mitigated or lawfully managed.

## 11. Integrity, Procurement and Anti-Corruption Controls

Minimum controls should include open and documented procurement; published eligibility and evaluation criteria; beneficial-ownership disclosure where lawful; conflict-of-interest declarations; separation of duties; competitive price checks; contract and change-order registers; independent engineering verification; payment controls tied to verified milestones; whistleblower protections; and regular independent audits.

Any allegation of fraud, diversion, coercion or serious safeguard breach should trigger a documented review and, where appropriate, suspension of the affected activity pending investigation.

## 12. Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning

A results framework should be agreed before implementation. Each project should define:
- Baseline, target, data source, frequency and responsible verifier for every indicator.
- Outputs, service outcomes and longer-term impact assumptions separately.
- Disaggregation only where lawful, safe and methodologically sound.
- Independent verification of major milestones and beneficiary feedback.
- Public reporting of budget, procurement, progress, delays, corrective actions and final evaluation, subject to legitimate privacy and security restrictions.

Illustrative cross-program indicators include infrastructure functionality, service reliability, employment outcomes, agricultural value retained, access to essential services, maintenance performance and community satisfaction. Numeric targets should be set only after baseline studies.

## 13. Key Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Proposed response |
|---|---|
| Security and access constraints | Context-specific security assessment, phased access and stop-work protocols |
| Legal uncertainty or land disputes | Independent title and rights review before site commitment |
| Corruption and procurement abuse | Competitive procurement, audit trails, disclosures and independent audit |
| Funding gaps and cost escalation | Stage-gated commitments, contingencies and realistic cash-flow planning |
| Climate and water stress | Climate screening, resilient design and water-balance analysis |
| Weak operations and maintenance | Qualified operators, lifecycle budgets, training and service-level agreements |
| Market-demand shortfalls | Independent demand studies, buyer validation and conservative forecasts |
| Social exclusion or harm | Community consultation, accessible grievance channels and safeguard monitoring |

## 14. Partnership Opportunities

Qualified partners may be invited to consider clearly defined roles, subject to their mandates and due diligence:

- Technical assistance for feasibility studies, engineering and safeguards.
- Grant support for public-benefit components and project preparation.
- Investment in commercially viable energy, processing, storage or logistics projects.
- Training partnerships with employers and accredited providers.
- Independent monitoring, audit and evaluation.
- Local implementation partnerships with qualified and lawfully authorized organizations.

Any partnership must be documented through written agreements defining scope, responsibilities, funding, compliance, data handling, dispute resolution and exit arrangements.

## 15. Immediate Next Steps

1. Identify and legally verify the program's sponsoring entity, ownership and authority to represent the initiative.
2. Establish a small independent technical advisory group with disclosed qualifications and conflicts of interest.
3. Select a limited number of candidate projects using published criteria and documented community consultation.
4. Commission baseline, pre-feasibility, land, market, climate and safeguards assessments.
5. Prepare project-specific budgets, implementation schedules and operating models.
6. Build a secure data room containing verified documents for partner due diligence.
7. Approach prospective partners with project-specific requests only after the supporting evidence is complete.

## 16. Conclusion

The proposed program offers a framework for preparing practical, accountable and sustainable reconstruction projects in Afghanistan. Its credibility will depend not on the size of headline funding figures, but on verified needs, sound feasibility, lawful authority, transparent procurement, independent oversight and the ability to operate and maintain completed assets.

The program therefore invites technical dialogue and project-preparation cooperation. Any financing or implementation decision should be made only after independent due diligence and written approvals.
