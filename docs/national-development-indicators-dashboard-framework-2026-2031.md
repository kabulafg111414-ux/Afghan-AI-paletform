# National Development Indicators and Progress Dashboard Framework (2026–2031)

**Status:** Proposed technical framework for consultation and project preparation  
**Program:** Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program (2026–2031)  
**Version:** 1.0

> **Important status note:** This document is a proposed measurement framework. It is not an approved national statistical system, an official government dashboard, or evidence that funding, projects, targets, or institutional commitments have been approved. No baseline, result, or target is invented here. Values must be populated only from documented and appropriately authorized sources.

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines how the program could measure implementation, outputs, outcomes, risks, and public accountability across sectors. It is intended to support:
- consistent project and sector reporting;
- evidence-based decisions and corrective action;
- transparent reporting of approved budgets, commitments, contracts, and actual expenditure;
- public communication that distinguishes verified facts from estimates and plans;
- learning and evaluation without overstating the program's contribution.

The dashboard should complement, not replace, official national statistics, ministry information systems, independent audits, or project-level monitoring.

## 2. Measurement principles

1. **No fabricated data:** never publish a value without a traceable source, reference period, method, and responsible owner.
2. **Separate status categories:** planned, proposed, approved, contracted, committed, disbursed, and spent are different states.
3. **Baseline before target:** establish and validate a baseline before approving numerical targets.
4. **Attribution discipline:** distinguish national trends from results directly delivered by a project or plausibly influenced by the program.
5. **Comparable definitions:** use stable definitions, units, time periods, and calculation methods.
6. **Independent verification:** higher-risk and material results should receive documented independent checks.
7. **Privacy and do-no-harm:** publish aggregated information; protect personal, medical, child, beneficiary, and commercially sensitive data.
8. **Accessible publication:** make the dashboard usable on mobile devices, in Dari, Pashto, and English where feasible, with text labels as well as colors.
9. **Revision transparency:** preserve prior published values and explain corrections.
10. **Equity with safeguards:** disaggregate only where lawful, safe, statistically meaningful, and privacy-protective.

## 3. Standard indicator record

Every indicator must have a complete metadata record before it is displayed as a verified result.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Indicator ID | Stable unique code, e.g. EDU-001 |
| Name and definition | Plain-language title and precise operational definition |
| Purpose | Decision or outcome the indicator informs |
| Level | Context, input, activity, output, outcome, or impact |
| Unit | AFN, USD, people, facilities, km, %, hours, etc. |
| Calculation | Numerator, denominator, exclusions, and formula |
| Scope | Geography, project, facility, or population covered |
| Disaggregation | Relevant categories, subject to safety and privacy |
| Baseline | Value, reference date, source, and verification status |
| Target | Value, target year, approval authority, and rationale |
| Actual | Value, reporting period, source, and publication date |
| Data source | Named dataset, record, survey, or administrative system |
| Method | Collection and estimation method, including sampling where relevant |
| Owner | Responsible data-producing organization or project role |
| Frequency | Monthly, quarterly, annual, or event-based |
| Verifier | Independent or designated quality reviewer |
| Confidence | Verified, provisionally checked, estimated, or unavailable |
| Limitations | Coverage gaps, uncertainty, comparability, and known bias |
| Version history | Date, prior value, revised value, reason, and approver |

A blank field must not be silently interpreted as zero. Use **Not yet measured**, **Not applicable**, **Not disclosed**, or **Data unavailable**, with an explanation.

## 4. Indicator families

The following are candidate indicator areas, not confirmed commitments or a claim that every indicator is currently measurable. Final selection should be based on relevance, data availability, cost, safety, and stakeholder review.

### 4.1 Economy and enterprise
- Real GDP growth and sector composition, only from an identified authoritative statistical source.
- Consumer price inflation and food-price trends, with source and period stated.
- Export value and composition, using a consistent trade methodology.
- New and surviving enterprises, with duplicate registration and informal-sector limitations disclosed.
- Private investment actually realized, separated from announced or proposed investment.
- Public and program expenditure, separated by approved budget, commitment, disbursement, and verified payment.

### 4.2 Employment and livelihoods
- Temporary and permanent jobs supported, reported separately.
- Full-time-equivalent (FTE) employment using a published hours-to-FTE method.
- Job duration, wages paid, and worker safety compliance where records are reliable.
- Training completion and verified employment after training.
- Youth, women, and disability-related breakdowns only where collection is lawful, safe, voluntary where appropriate, and privacy-protective.
- Avoid double-counting workers across contractors and projects.

### 4.3 Education and skills
- Enrollment, attendance, retention, and completion, with grade and geography.
- Learning outcomes from a documented assessment method.
- School readiness and availability of essential learning materials.
- Teacher attendance and professional development completion.
- Vocational course completion and post-training employment, with follow-up period stated.
- Accessibility of learning environments and reasonable accommodation, where safely assessed.

### 4.4 Health
- Functionality and readiness of supported health facilities against a defined checklist.
- Availability of essential medicines, using a named tracer-medicine list.
- Service utilization by service type and period.
- Referral completion and access time, where reliable records exist.
- Health workforce availability and training.
- Patient safety and quality indicators using aggregated, privacy-protective reporting. Never publish identifiable patient data.

### 4.5 Water, sanitation, and agriculture
- Population or households with access to safely managed or otherwise clearly defined drinking-water services.
- Water quality compliance against the stated standard and testing protocol.
- Irrigated area rehabilitated and area with verified reliable water delivery, reported separately.
- Agricultural yield by crop and season, with source and method.
- Post-harvest loss using a documented sampling approach.
- Sanitation and waste-service coverage, with service-quality definitions.

### 4.6 Energy
- New connections and people gaining access, with unique-counting rules.
- Hours of service and outage frequency for supported systems.
- Installed capacity versus commissioned and operational capacity.
- Renewable generation actually delivered, not merely installed capacity.
- Operation, maintenance, affordability, and system reliability.

### 4.7 Transport and infrastructure
- Road length rehabilitated or constructed, with engineering acceptance records.
- Road condition and seasonal accessibility using a defined rating method.
- Travel-time changes measured on comparable routes and periods.
- Infrastructure assets completed, operational, and maintained, reported separately.
- Safety, environmental, land, and resettlement compliance where relevant.

### 4.8 Environment, climate, and disaster resilience
- Water, soil, and air-quality measurements with sampling locations and methods.
- Waste collected, treated, recycled, or safely disposed, each as a separate measure.
- Energy or emissions indicators only where a recognized accounting boundary and method exist.
- Infrastructure screened for climate and disaster risks.
- Preparedness plans tested and corrective actions completed.
- Avoid unsupported claims of emissions reductions or climate impact.

### 4.9 Inclusion and community accountability
- Projects completing documented stakeholder consultation before approval.
- Facilities meeting a defined accessibility standard.
- Grievances received, acknowledged, resolved, and overdue, using anonymized aggregate data.
- Community feedback and satisfaction, with survey method and response rate.
- Benefit access across relevant locations and groups, without exposing individuals or vulnerable communities.

### 4.10 Governance, procurement, and public finance
- Procurement packages competitively tendered, with denominator and exceptions disclosed.
- Contracts and award notices published, subject to lawful confidentiality exceptions.
- Contract variations, cost overruns, and delivery delays against approved baselines.
- Audit findings issued, accepted, and closed, with severity classification.
- Corrective actions completed by due date.
- Budget execution reported separately as approved, committed, disbursed, and verified expenditure.
- Conflicts-of-interest declarations and compliance checks completed by responsible personnel.

## 5. Context indicators versus program results

The dashboard must visibly distinguish:

- **National context:** economy-wide or country-wide trends that may be affected by many factors.
- **Program outputs:** goods, services, or infrastructure directly delivered and accepted under a documented project.
- **Program outcomes:** changes among intended users or systems measured with a defined method.
- **Long-term impact:** broader changes that require stronger evaluation and cannot normally be attributed to one program from simple before-and-after comparisons.

Use cautious language such as “associated with” or “contributed to” only where the evaluation design supports it. Do not claim that a national trend was caused by this program merely because it occurred during the same period.

## 6. Baselines and target setting

For each selected indicator:
1. confirm the definition and intended decision use;
2. identify the authoritative or best available data source;
3. assess coverage, quality, access rights, and collection cost;
4. establish the baseline and reference period;
5. document limitations and confidence;
6. propose a target with a transparent rationale and resource assumptions;
7. obtain approval from the designated governance authority;
8. publish the baseline, target, approval date, and methodology together.

Until this process is completed, display **Baseline pending** and **Target pending** rather than inventing values. Targets must be realistic, funded, time-bound, and consistent with safeguards.

## 7. Dashboard structure

### Public view
- Program purpose, geographic scope, and current verified status.
- Approved projects and their stage: concept, preparation, approved, procurement, implementation, completed, or suspended.
- Sector summaries with verified outputs and reporting dates.
- Financial summary separating approved budget, commitments, disbursements, and verified expenditure.
- Procurement notices, contract summaries, audit summaries, and public reports where lawful.
- Data definitions, sources, update dates, limitations, and correction log.
- Grievance channels that do not expose complainants.

### Internal management view
- Project-level indicator records and source evidence.
- Milestones, schedule variance, cost variance, and risk register.
- Data-quality flags and overdue submissions.
- Contract, safeguard, audit, and corrective-action tracking.
- Forecasts clearly labeled as forecasts, not actual results.
- Role-based access and audit logs for changes.

Public and internal views must use the same underlying definitions. Sensitive personal or security information must remain restricted.

## 8. Status and exception rules

Use four plain-language status classes:
- **On track:** performance meets an approved threshold for the reporting period.
- **Watch:** a documented risk or variance requires attention.
- **Off track:** an approved threshold has been breached or a critical milestone is materially delayed.
- **No data:** evidence is missing, late, or not sufficiently reliable.

Thresholds must be approved for each indicator based on its baseline, target, risk, and reporting frequency. Do not assign colors or statuses by intuition. Always display text labels and, where color is used, use accessible contrast and patterns or icons as a second cue.

A critical safeguard breach, suspected fraud, serious safety incident, or material data-integrity concern should trigger a separate escalation process rather than being hidden inside an average score.

## 9. Data collection and quality assurance

Minimum controls:
- Maintain a source register with owner, access authority, collection date, and retention rules.
- Use standard forms and controlled definitions across projects.
- Apply range checks, duplicate checks, denominator checks, and period checks.
- Reconcile reported outputs with invoices, engineering acceptance, facility records, or other primary evidence as appropriate.
- Conduct risk-based spot checks and independent verification.
- Keep an immutable or access-controlled change log for published values.
- Record missing data and reasons; never substitute zero.
- Document estimates, sampling error, and revisions.
- Separate data entry, approval, and publication permissions.
- Provide a correction and appeal channel for affected reporting entities.

## 10. Privacy, safeguarding, and responsible publication

- Collect only data necessary for a defined program purpose.
- Apply data minimization, access controls, encryption in transit and at rest where systems support it, and retention schedules.
- Do not publish names, phone numbers, identity documents, individual health records, exact household locations, or identifiable case narratives.
- Suppress small cells or combinations that could re-identify people or expose vulnerable groups.
- Obtain appropriate consent and approvals where required; explain how information will be used.
- Establish safe referral and incident procedures for safeguarding disclosures.
- Review maps and geographic detail for security and do-no-harm risks before publication.
- Do not imply that a public dashboard is a channel for emergency response.

## 11. Reporting calendar

| Product | Suggested frequency | Minimum content |
|---|---|---|
| Project implementation update | Monthly | Milestones, risks, procurement, expenditure status, data gaps |
| Sector performance review | Quarterly | Outputs, outcomes, variance analysis, corrective actions |
| Public program report | Quarterly | Verified highlights, financial status, delays, limitations |
| Annual results and financial report | Annually | Results against approved targets, audited financial information where available, lessons |
| Independent evaluation | At defined milestones | Relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability, and impact where evaluable |

The schedule is proposed and should be aligned with actual institutional capacity and approved reporting obligations.

## 12. Roles and accountability

- **Program steering authority:** approves indicator set, targets, publication policy, and major changes.
- **Program coordination unit:** maintains the master indicator register and reporting calendar.
- **Project implementing entities:** collect and submit source evidence and explain variances.
- **Sector technical leads:** validate definitions, methods, and sector interpretation.
- **Finance and procurement functions:** reconcile financial and contract information.
- **Safeguards and data-protection focal points:** review privacy, inclusion, and do-no-harm risks.
- **Independent verifier or auditor:** checks material results and control effectiveness within an agreed mandate.
- **Public communications function:** publishes only approved, evidence-backed summaries and correction notices.

No role should certify its own work without an appropriate review or separation of duties.

## 13. Minimum data schema for spreadsheet or system implementation

Recommended columns:

`indicator_id, indicator_name, definition, level, sector, unit, geography, disaggregation, baseline_value, baseline_date, baseline_source, target_value, target_year, target_approval_ref, actual_value, period_start, period_end, actual_source, method, owner, frequency, verifier, confidence_status, limitations, last_updated, version`

Store numeric values separately from formatted display strings. Include currency, price year, exchange-rate basis, and nominal/real treatment for financial indicators. Preserve source references and evidence securely; do not expose restricted source files publicly.

## 14. Implementation sequence

**Stage 1 — Design:** appoint owners, confirm governance, select a small core indicator set, and approve definitions.

**Stage 2 — Baseline:** map sources, assess quality, collect baselines, and document gaps.

**Stage 3 — Pilot:** test reporting with a limited number of prepared projects; verify data and revise forms.

**Stage 4 — Publish:** release a public dashboard only after privacy, security, accessibility, and data-quality reviews.

**Stage 5 — Expand:** add sectors and projects when reporting capacity and evidence quality are adequate.

**Stage 6 — Evaluate:** commission periodic independent reviews and publish management responses.

## 15. Go-live checklist

- [ ] Governance authority and publication owner formally designated.
- [ ] Indicator dictionary reviewed by sector specialists.
- [ ] Baselines have source records and reference dates.
- [ ] Targets are approved and linked to feasible resources.
- [ ] Financial terms are clearly distinguished.
- [ ] Data owners and submission deadlines are assigned.
- [ ] Verification and audit arrangements are documented.
- [ ] Privacy, safeguarding, security, and accessibility reviews completed.
- [ ] Public correction and grievance channels are functioning.
- [ ] Dashboard labels clearly identify estimates, missing data, and provisional values.
- [ ] No unsupported logos, endorsements, partner claims, or funding claims appear.

## 16. Decisions required before operational use

The program's authorized governance body must decide:
1. the legal and institutional mandate for collecting and publishing data;
2. the approved core indicator set and responsible data owners;
3. baseline sources and permitted access arrangements;
4. target-setting and approval authority;
5. public disclosure, privacy, retention, and security rules;
6. independent verification scope and funding;
7. the technology platform, hosting, maintenance, and operating budget.

Until these decisions are made and implemented, this framework remains a planning document and must not be described as an operational national dashboard.
