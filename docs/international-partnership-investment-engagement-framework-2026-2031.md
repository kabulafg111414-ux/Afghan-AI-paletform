# International Partnership and Investment Engagement Framework
## Afghanistan National Reconstruction & Sustainable Development Program | 2026–2031

**Document status:** Proposed engagement framework and non-binding templates  
**Purpose:** Guide transparent, lawful and evidence-based engagement with development institutions, donors, investors, private companies, technical partners and civil society.

> **Important status statement:** This document is not an offer of securities, a solicitation of investment, a financing commitment, an official government proposal or evidence of endorsement by any institution. No organization named as a potential audience is represented as a partner. Any partnership requires independent due diligence, verified legal authority, written approval and signed documentation by authorized parties.

---

## 1. Partnership objectives

The program may explore partnerships to:
- Prepare and independently assess priority infrastructure and public-service projects.
- Mobilize grant funding for technical assistance and public-benefit activities.
- Identify responsible private investment for projects with credible commercial models.
- Strengthen local technical, institutional and workforce capacity.
- Improve environmental, social, financial and procurement standards.
- Support monitoring, evaluation, independent audit and knowledge transfer.

Engagement must be project-specific. A general expression of interest must not be presented as approval or financing.

## 2. Potential partner categories

| Partner category | Potential contribution | Key checks before engagement |
|---|---|---|
| Multilateral development institutions | Technical standards, analytical support, possible financing subject to mandate and eligibility | Country/program eligibility, legal mandate, safeguards, procurement and debt rules |
| UN entities and international organizations | Technical assistance, humanitarian/development coordination where within mandate | Mandate fit, access, neutrality, safeguarding, partner due diligence |
| Bilateral development agencies | Grants, technical cooperation and capacity building subject to country priorities | Eligibility, foreign-policy and legal requirements, funding windows |
| Development finance institutions | Debt, guarantees or blended finance for eligible projects | Debt sustainability, environmental/social standards, financial viability |
| Private companies and operators | Engineering, construction, equipment, operations and technology | Beneficial ownership, track record, sanctions/legal screening, conflicts and capacity |
| Institutional and impact investors | Equity or other risk capital where suitable | Commercial case, risk allocation, exit, governance and investor protections |
| Universities and research bodies | Feasibility studies, research, training and independent evaluation | Academic independence, methodology and data protection |
| Local communities and civil society | Local knowledge, consultation, oversight and feedback | Inclusive participation, safety, independence and non-coercion |

These are categories for exploration only. Eligibility and interest must be confirmed directly with each organization.

## 3. Partnership principles

All engagement should follow:
1. **Legal authority:** The proposing entity must be legally established and authorized to negotiate.
2. **Transparency:** Disclose the program's status, ownership, governance and funding accurately.
3. **No implied endorsement:** Use partner names and logos only with written authorization.
4. **Integrity:** Apply anti-bribery, conflict-of-interest, procurement and beneficial-ownership checks.
5. **Safeguards:** Respect applicable environmental, social, labor, human-rights and safeguarding requirements.
6. **Local value:** Build local skills, supplier capacity and sustainable operating arrangements where appropriate.
7. **Financial responsibility:** Distinguish grants, loans, equity, guarantees, pledges and received funds.
8. **Data protection:** Share only necessary information through secure, authorized channels.
9. **Independent review:** Permit appropriate technical, financial and safeguard due diligence.
10. **No guarantee:** Do not promise returns, repayment, sovereign support, permits or security conditions that are not legally established.

## 4. Engagement stages and decision gates

| Stage | Activity | Required evidence / output |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Internal readiness | Verify entity, ownership, authority, governance and project status | Legal and governance pack |
| 1. Partner mapping | Identify institutions whose mandates align with a specific project | Documented eligibility and mandate review |
| 2. Initial contact | Send a concise, factual non-binding concept note | Authorized introductory letter |
| 3. Expression of interest | Clarify potential scope without implying commitment | Written record of non-binding discussions |
| 4. Due diligence | Review legal, technical, financial, integrity and safeguard matters | Due-diligence register and findings |
| 5. Project preparation | Commission feasibility, design, cost and operating analysis | Investment-ready project dossier |
| 6. Negotiation | Agree scope, responsibilities, risks, funding and dispute procedures | Reviewed term sheet or draft agreement |
| 7. Authorization | Obtain required board, regulator, government, donor and lender approvals | Formal approvals and signed agreement |
| 8. Implementation | Manage procurement, delivery, safeguards and reporting | Contract and performance reports |
| 9. Evaluation and close-out | Verify outputs, outcomes, assets and obligations | Independent completion and evaluation report |

No public announcement should describe a partnership as concluded before the relevant agreement is signed and any required approvals are effective.

## 5. Standard partnership information package

Before approaching a prospective partner, prepare and quality-check:

### A. Institutional documents
- Certificate of registration and current legal status.
- Governing documents and ownership information.
- Board or authorized decision-making records.
- Authorized signatory list and delegation of authority.
- Governance, procurement, finance and anti-corruption policies.
- Audited financial statements, if available; otherwise clearly disclose that they are not available.
- Tax, licensing and regulatory documentation as applicable.

### B. Program documents
- Program overview and clearly stated concept-stage status.
- Sector strategy and project prioritization methodology.
- Transparency and accountability framework.
- Environmental and social safeguards framework.
- Monitoring, evaluation and reporting framework.
- Risk register and implementation capacity assessment.

### C. Project-specific documents
- Project concept note and verified need.
- Location, land status and required permits.
- Feasibility study and options analysis.
- Technical design and bill of quantities.
- Independent cost estimate and estimate date.
- CAPEX, OPEX and lifecycle funding plan.
- Economic and financial appraisal.
- Procurement and implementation schedule.
- Climate, environmental and social assessments.
- Operator, ownership and maintenance arrangements.

Do not send confidential personal data, bank credentials, security-sensitive location details or unverified claims in an introductory package.

## 6. Partner due-diligence checklist

### Proposing entity
- Is the legal entity registered and in good standing?
- Does it have authority to propose and negotiate the project?
- Are its owners, controllers and authorized representatives verified?
- Are governance and financial controls documented?
- Are there unresolved litigation, sanctions, insolvency or integrity issues that must be disclosed?
- Can it demonstrate delivery capacity and appropriate professional qualifications?

### Prospective partner
- Is the organization genuine and authorized to act?
- Does the proposed activity fit its mandate and eligibility rules?
- Are beneficial owners, directors and intermediaries appropriately screened?
- Are conflicts, politically exposed person risks and sanctions requirements addressed as required by applicable law?
- Are funding sources and conditions transparent?
- Are environmental, social, labor and safeguarding standards compatible?
- Are fees, commissions, agents and intermediaries disclosed and justified?

### Project
- Is the need independently supported?
- Are land rights, access and permits verified?
- Are costs, demand and benefits evidence-based?
- Is the project affordable over its full lifecycle?
- Are risks allocated to parties able to manage them?
- Is the procurement approach fair and competitive?
- Are monitoring, grievance and audit arrangements credible?

A checklist does not replace formal legal, financial, technical or sanctions advice.

## 7. Financing structures and appropriate use

| Instrument | Potential fit | Essential conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Grant | Feasibility, training, public goods, essential services and safeguards | Eligibility, approved purpose, reporting and audit |
| Technical assistance | Design, institutional capacity and independent review | Clear scope, deliverables and independence |
| Concessional loan | Eligible projects with a sustainable repayment source | Debt sustainability, lawful borrowing authority and full disclosure |
| Commercial loan | Projects with reliable cash flow and debt capacity | Market-based risk assessment, affordability and authorized borrower |
| Equity investment | Commercial operations with investable governance and returns | Valuation, ownership rights, exit terms and risk disclosure |
| Guarantee / risk-sharing | Eligible projects where risk allocation is demonstrably improved | Legal authority, contingent-liability disclosure and transparent pricing |
| Public-private partnership | Long-term service or infrastructure delivery where value for money is proven | Affordability, competitive process, fiscal-risk assessment and contract transparency |
| In-kind contribution | Equipment, expertise or services | Independent valuation, ownership, quality and maintenance arrangements |

Do not describe a loan as a grant, a pledge as received cash, or a proposed PPP as an approved contract. Do not promise sovereign guarantees or public repayment without lawful authorization.

## 10. Non-binding memorandum of understanding (MoU) outline

Any MoU must be reviewed by qualified legal counsel and signed only by verified authorized representatives.

1. Title, date and identity of parties.
2. Background and purpose.
3. Definitions and non-binding status.
4. Proposed areas of cooperation.
5. Roles and responsibilities.
6. Focal points and communication protocol.
7. Information sharing and confidentiality.
8. Data protection and intellectual property.
9. Costs and expenses; no funding obligation unless separately agreed.
10. Integrity, anti-bribery and conflict-of-interest requirements.
11. Environmental, social and safeguarding principles.
12. Public statements, name and logo use.
13. Compliance with applicable laws and institutional policies.
14. Duration, review, amendment and termination.
15. Dispute discussion and applicable legal provisions, if any.
16. Provisions intended to be binding, if any, clearly identified.
17. Signatures and evidence of authority.

**Model non-binding clause:**  
“This memorandum records the parties' present intentions for discussion and cooperation only. Except for clauses expressly stated to be binding, it does not create a legally enforceable obligation to provide funding, award a contract, make an investment, guarantee repayment or proceed with a project. Any funded activity is subject to due diligence, internal approvals, applicable law and a separate definitive written agreement signed by authorized representatives.”

## 11. Investor protection and risk allocation

Any investment discussion should transparently address:
- Legal identity and authority of the project sponsor.
- Ownership and control of project assets.
- Permits, land rights and access.
- Currency, convertibility and payment risks.
- Construction, completion and performance risks.
- Demand, tariff and collection risks.
- Political, regulatory and force-majeure risks.
- Environmental, social and community risks.
- Insurance and liability.
- Dispute resolution and enforcement.
- Exit, transfer and termination rights.
- Disclosure, audit and anti-corruption obligations.

Risk should be allocated to the party best able to control or mitigate it. Avoid hidden guarantees, undisclosed side letters, informal commissions or promises that cannot be legally delivered.

## 12. Protection of Afghanistan's public interest

For every proposed partnership, assess:
- Whether public assets or natural resources are involved and whether the transaction is legally authorized.
- Whether obligations create direct or contingent public liabilities.
- Whether pricing, tariffs, land use and service access are fair and affordable.
- Whether local employment and skills transfer are measurable and realistic.
- Whether local suppliers can compete fairly and meet quality requirements.
- Whether environmental damage, displacement or livelihood impacts are avoided or properly mitigated.
- Whether contract terms, beneficial ownership and public obligations can be disclosed.
- Whether the agreement preserves lawful oversight, audit and remedies.

No natural resource, public asset, land right, tax concession or sovereign obligation should be offered or promised without specific legal authority and transparent approval.

## 13. Communications and claims protocol

Use accurate status language:
- “Concept under preparation” when only an initial concept exists.
- “Technical dialogue requested” after an introductory request is sent.
- “Discussion held” only after a meeting occurred.
- “Expression of interest received” only when documented.
- “Due diligence underway” only when formally initiated.
- “Agreement signed” only when executed by authorized parties.
- “Funding committed” only when a binding, authorized commitment exists.
- “Funds received” only after funds clear into an authorized account.

Do not use institutional logos, imply endorsement, publish partner names as confirmed, or announce funding before written authorization and verification.

## 14. First 90-day readiness work plan

| Period | Workstream | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–15 | Verify legal entity, ownership, authority and governance | Legal readiness register |
| Days 16–30 | Select one or two candidate projects and confirm evidence gaps | Prioritized project concept notes |
| Days 31–45 | Commission or plan independent feasibility, safeguard and cost work | Terms of reference and preparation plan |
| Days 46–60 | Complete institutional policies and due-diligence package | Governance and compliance data room |
| Days 61–75 | Map potential partners by mandate and eligibility | Evidence-based partner matrix |
| Days 76–90 | Send tailored, non-binding requests where authorized | Contact log and next-step register |

The timetable is indicative and depends on resources, access, legal status and availability of qualified independent experts.

## 15. Partnership register

Maintain a controlled register with:
- Organization name and official source used to verify it.
- Contact person and role, if verified.
- Mandate and eligibility notes.
- Project or sector of relevance.
- Date and nature of contact.
- Documents shared and confidentiality status.
- Current engagement status.
- Due-diligence actions and responsible owner.
- Conflicts, risks and decisions.
- Next step and deadline.

A name in this register means only that an organization has been mapped for possible engagement; it does not mean that the organization is interested, involved or a partner.

## 16. Final disclosure

This framework and its templates are for planning and preliminary dialogue. They do not create an investment offer, public borrowing authority, partnership, government approval, donor commitment or institutional endorsement. Before any external circulation, replace placeholders with verified information, confirm the legal status and authority of the proposing entity, obtain legal review and approve the communication through the proper governance process.
